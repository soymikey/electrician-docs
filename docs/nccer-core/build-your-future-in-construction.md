# Module 00100: Build Your Future in Construction

This page organizes Module 00100 from the Sixth Edition into 31 active-recall cards. Answer each **Front** question before expanding the **Back** to check your response.

:::info[How to review]
Mark a card **Know** if you can explain it fully and give an example without looking. Mark it **Unsure** if you remember only part of the answer, and **Do not know** if you cannot answer. Repeat the **Unsure** and **Do not know** cards first.
:::

:::caution[Textbook data]
Industry, salary, and employment figures in the textbook are a Sixth Edition course snapshot, not current real-time data.
:::

## Section 1: Overview of the Construction Industry

### 1.1.0 What Is Construction?

**Front:** Besides building at a job site, what work does construction include?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** It includes design, planning, management, repair, maintenance, and demolition. Construction is both a science and an art, requiring technical skill, creativity, and cooperation among many crafts.

</details>

### 1.1.1 State of the Industry

**Front:** How does the textbook describe the construction industry's size and outlook?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Construction is a major industry that employs millions of workers. Private construction and investment in aging infrastructure will continue to create demand for workers.

</details>

### 1.1.2 Growing Demand for Skilled Craft Professionals

**Front:** Why is there a shortage of skilled craft professionals?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Many Baby Boomers are approaching retirement, while too few younger workers are entering construction. Employers therefore need to recruit and train new workers.

</details>

### 1.1.3 Common Construction Industry Myths

**Front:** What are six common myths about the construction industry?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** The claims that construction requires no skill, pays only minimum wage, is unsuitable for women, does not use technology, is extremely dangerous, and always harms the environment are misleading. Modern construction relies on training, credentials, technology, and strong safety practices.

</details>

### 1.2.0 History of Construction

**Front:** What technological changes have advanced the construction industry?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Construction progressed from hand tools to steam-powered machinery and then to CAD, BIM, automation, and other digital technologies.

</details>

### 1.2.1 Famous Construction Examples

**Front:** Which famous projects does the textbook identify, and what do they demonstrate?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** The Eiffel Tower, Empire State Building, Golden Gate Bridge, Hoover Dam, and Burj Khalifa demonstrate breakthroughs in materials, design, and construction across different eras.

</details>

### 1.2.2 Important Construction Innovations and Inventions

**Front:** What problems do PPE, CAD, BIM, and VR each help solve?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** PPE protects workers; CAD supports accurate drawing and design; BIM coordinates building information; and VR lets teams experience and evaluate a design before construction.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
A major cause of the skilled-worker shortage is Baby Boomer retirement. The textbook answer for women's earnings is about 99% of men's earnings. Steam-powered machinery was the important nineteenth-century invention that increased construction speed.
:::

## Section 2: Benefits of a Career in Construction

### 2.1.0 Making a Difference

**Front:** How can a construction career make a lasting difference in society?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Construction professionals create and maintain homes, schools, hospitals, roads, and infrastructure that communities may use for many years.

</details>

### 2.1.1 Providing Essential Services

**Front:** Why may construction workers be considered first responders after a disaster?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** They help restore electricity, water, roads, housing, and other essential services and facilities.

</details>

### 2.1.2 Working to Help the Environment

**Front:** In what ways can construction professionals help the environment?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** They can use energy-efficient systems, renewable energy, green construction practices, material recycling, and sustainable building programs such as LEED.

</details>

### 2.2.0 Construction Career Benefits and Opportunities

**Front:** What three main types of benefits can a construction career provide?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Competitive earnings, entry routes that can reduce education debt, and clear opportunities for advancement and geographic mobility.

</details>

### 2.2.1 Competitive Salaries

**Front:** Why should you look beyond base annual salary when comparing construction pay?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Total compensation may include overtime, bonuses, and other benefits, and it varies by craft, skill level, location, and experience.

</details>

### 2.2.2 Starting Out Debt-Free

**Front:** How can apprenticeship help a trainee reduce education debt?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Many construction crafts do not require a four-year degree. Through an `earn while you learn` program, trainees receive instruction while earning wages on the job.

</details>

### 2.2.3 Career Advancement and Mobility

**Front:** Which abilities support advancement in construction, and why are these careers mobile?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Workers need communication, teamwork, decision-making, and leadership in addition to craft skill. These skills are needed in many locations, making it possible to move where work is available.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
Restoring essential services after a disaster is True. A key benefit of craft training is that you can `earn while you learn`. Career mobility comes from demand for craft skills in many locations.
:::

## Section 3: Construction Craft Careers

### 3.1.0 Construction Industry Sectors

**Front:** What are the four major sectors of the construction industry?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Residential, Commercial, Industrial, and Heavy Civil/Infrastructure. A contractor usually works directly for the project owner and hires subcontractors to complete specialized work.

</details>

### 3.2.0 Types of Construction Careers

**Front:** What should you compare when choosing a construction career?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Compare job duties, required skills, work environment, training time, credentials, earnings, outlook, and your personal interests.

</details>

### 3.2.1 Carpenter

**Front:** What is the difference between a rough carpenter and a finish carpenter?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A rough carpenter works on framing, forms, and structural components. A finish carpenter produces visible, detailed work such as door and window trim, molding, and stairs.

</details>

### 3.2.2 Electrician

**Front:** What systems do electricians install, and what qualifications do they commonly need?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Electricians install and maintain power, lighting, control, communication, and low-voltage systems. Most states require a license, and electricians must understand national, state, and local electrical codes.

</details>

### 3.2.3 Welder

**Front:** What do welders do besides joining metal, and what specialties are available?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Welders also cut metal. Specialties include pipe welding, stainless-steel welding, weld inspection, underwater welding, and robotic welding.

</details>

### 3.2.4 Pipefitter

**Front:** What is the main focus of a pipefitter's work?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Pipefitters build and maintain commercial and industrial piping systems that carry water, gases, chemicals, fuels, and other materials. Their work requires print reading, measuring, welding, and assembly skills.

</details>

### 3.2.5 Heavy Equipment Operator

**Front:** What is a heavy equipment operator's central responsibility?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** The operator must safely run equipment such as excavators, bulldozers, and loaders while inspecting the machine and watching for blind spots, underground utilities, and nearby people.

</details>

### 3.2.6 Crane Operator

**Front:** Why does a crane operator need mathematics, trigonometry, and physics?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** The operator must determine maximum loads based on lifting radius, angle, and equipment configuration. Operators also commonly need the appropriate certification or license.

</details>

### 3.2.7 Ironworker

**Front:** What does an ironworker primarily build?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Ironworkers install steel beams, columns, and reinforcing materials to form the structural frames of buildings, bridges, and other structures. The work requires strength, balance, and the ability to work at height.

</details>

### 3.2.8 HVAC Technician

**Front:** Which systems and problems does an HVAC technician handle?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** HVAC technicians work on heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, and refrigeration systems, including related motors, pumps, fans, thermostats, electrical components, and controls.

</details>

### 3.2.9 Project Manager

**Front:** What does a project manager oversee, and why is college education commonly required?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Project managers oversee budgets, schedules, personnel, quality, and coordination. The role requires management, business, communication, and decision-making knowledge, and commonly calls for a related bachelor's degree plus field experience.

</details>

### 3.2.10 More Construction Careers

**Front:** Where can you research crafts beyond those introduced in the textbook?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** NCCER provides training information for more than 45 crafts. [Build Your Future](https://www.byf.org/) offers information about career paths, salaries, and regional demand.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
Schools, government buildings, hotels, and shopping centers belong to the Commercial sector. The contractor usually works directly for the project owner. Entry into most crafts requires a high school diploma or equivalent. The crane operator calculates lifting loads.
:::

## Section 4: Starting Your Construction Career

### 4.1.0 Career and Technical Education

**Front:** How do CTE programs help students enter the construction industry?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** They build career readiness through hands-on practice, career exploration, and nontechnical skills such as communication, time management, and critical thinking.

</details>

### 4.2.0 Craft Training and Apprenticeships

**Front:** What is the typical apprenticeship structure described in the textbook?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** It is about 80% on-the-job learning and 20% classroom instruction, usually lasting two to four years. A typical year includes about 2,000 hours of OJL and 144 hours of classroom instruction.

</details>

### 4.3.0 Community Colleges and Universities

**Front:** Which goals are community colleges and four-year universities best suited to support?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Community colleges offer a faster route to associate degrees, industry credentials, and job skills. Universities are more appropriate for engineering, design, and management careers.

</details>

### 4.3.1 Community College

**Front:** What options are available after completing a community college program?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A graduate may enter an apprenticeship, go directly to work, or apply coursework and credits toward a university degree.

</details>

### 4.3.2 Four-Year Universities

**Front:** What are the advantages and limitations of a four-year university education?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** The advantage is deeper education in engineering, architecture, or construction management. Limitations include higher cost, and a degree does not guarantee employment; field experience remains important.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
CTE emphasizes hands-on learning. Apprenticeship or craft training commonly takes two to four years. The statement that a college degree guarantees a construction job after graduation is False.
:::
