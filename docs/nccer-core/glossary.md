## Industry, Careers and Construction Foundations {#topic-1}

<a id="term-11"></a>

**Apprenticeship/craft training:** A program in which you gain valuable skills on the job under the instruction of more experienced professionals, often making money as you obtain training.

<a id="term-13"></a>

**Architect:** A qualified, licensed person who creates and designs drawings for a construction project.

<a id="term-21"></a>

**Beams:** Large, horizontal structural members made of concrete, steel, stone, wood, or other structural material to provide support above a large opening.

<a id="term-37"></a>

**Career and technical education (CTE):** Programs offered in high school and technical education centers that allow students to explore career options available in construction.

<a id="term-47"></a>

**Clean rooms:** A room or area that is used for scientific research or specialized processes, such as the manufacture of pharmaceuticals and semiconductors. The cleanliness level of a clean room is generally based on the number of particles measured per cubic meter of area.

<a id="term-60"></a>

**Contractor:** Oversees many parts of a construction project, including resource management, budgeting, code adherence, quality assurance, and construction materials.

<a id="term-64"></a>

**Craft professional:** A trained and skilled individual who designs and builds things. Craft professionals conform to the technical and ethical standards of their trade.

<a id="term-63"></a>

**Craft/trade:** A general term referring to one of the many construction specialties.

<a id="term-65"></a>

**Crew leader:** Also called foreman, this supervisory role oversees a crew of craft professionals. It is the crew leaders' job to make sure that work is completed correctly and on time. Crew leaders are responsible for the safety and work of those under them.

<a id="term-66"></a>

**Cross-bracing:** Braces (metal or wood) placed diagonally from the bottom of one rail to the top of another rail to add support to a structure.

<a id="term-85"></a>

**Drywall:** A large, flat board made of layers of fiberboard and gypsum used primarily for wall construction and finishing. Drywall, also known as sheet rock and wallboard, is typically manufactured in 4' × 8' panels and readily accepts paint.

<a id="term-92"></a>

**Engineers:** People who apply scientific principles in design and construction.

<a id="term-114"></a>

**Green construction:** Construction practices that involve the creation of structures that adhere to sustainability principles.

<a id="term-115"></a>

**Grilles:** HVAC components that pull air from a room and return it to the heating or cooling system. They are different from a register in that they do not have a damper to control airflow.

<a id="term-132"></a>

**HVAC:** Heating, ventilating, and air conditioning.

<a id="term-138"></a>

**Infrastructure:** The transportation, water, electrical, and telecommunications systems that allow communities and countries to operate.

<a id="term-145"></a>

**Joists:** Lengths of wood or steel that usually support floors, ceilings, or a roof. Roof joists will be at the same angle as the roof itself, while floor and ceiling joists are usually horizontal.

<a id="term-148"></a>

**Lath:** A thin, flat strip of wood used to form latticework or a foundation for wall plaster.

<a id="term-157"></a>

**Loadbearing:** Carrying a significant amount of weight and/or providing necessary structural support. A loadbearing wall is typically carrying some portion of the roof's weight and cannot be removed without risking structural failure or collapse.

<a id="term-207"></a>

**Project manager:** Oversees the planning and delivery of construction projects on time and within budget.

<a id="term-208"></a>

**Project owner:** The initiator of the project who usually finances the building endeavor.

<a id="term-210"></a>

**Punch list:** A written list that identifies deficiencies requiring correction at completion.

<a id="term-214"></a>

**Rafter:** One of a series of beams that extend from the perimeter of a building to the high point of a roof, providing structural support for the roof above.

<a id="term-219"></a>

**Registers:** HVAC components that direct air from the heating or cooling system into a room. A damper in the register is used to control the airflow.

<a id="term-248"></a>

**Site superintendent:** Manages day-to-day activities on a construction site and supervises work performed by the subcontractors.

<a id="term-267"></a>

**Stud:** A vertical support inside a wall to which the wall finish material attaches. The base of a stud rests on a horizontal baseplate, and a horizontal cap plate rests on top of a series of studs.

<a id="term-268"></a>

**Subcontractor/specialty contractor:** Typically employs craft professionals who have specialized skills needed to complete a particular part of the construction project (e.g., HVAC, electrical, plumbing).

<a id="term-270"></a>

**Sustainability:** The practice of minimizing negative impacts to Earth's climate and natural environment.

<a id="term-292"></a>

**Virtual reality (VR):** Provides a 3D model of a construction site and allows users to directly immerse themselves into the virtual space. VR enables multiple people to envision the final product, feel what it is like to be in the space, and identify any issues before work begins.

## Jobsite Safety and Protection {#topic-2}

<a id="term-2"></a>

**Accident:** According to the US Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), an unplanned event that results in personal injury and/or property damage.

<a id="term-7"></a>

**Amphetamine:** A class of drugs that causes mental stimulation and feelings of euphoria.

<a id="term-19"></a>

**Barbiturate:** A class of drugs that induces relaxation, slowing the body's ability to react.

<a id="term-34"></a>

**Cannabinoids:** A diverse category of chemical substances that repress neurotransmitter releases in the brain. Cannabinoids have a variety of sources; some are created naturally by the human body, while others come from cannabis (marijuana). Still others are synthetic.

<a id="term-41"></a>

**Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD):** Lung disease(s) that results in the obstruction of lung airflow and interferes with normal breathing. Chronic bronchitis and emphysema generally fall under the diagnosis of COPD.

<a id="term-49"></a>

**Combustible:** Capable of easily igniting and rapidly burning; used to describe a fuel with a flash point at or above 100°F (38°C).

<a id="term-50"></a>

**Competent person:** A person who is capable of identifying existing and predictable hazards in the surroundings or working conditions that are unsanitary, hazardous, or dangerous to employees, and who has authorization to take prompt corrective measures to eliminate them. Also, an individual capable of identifying existing and predictable hazards in the surroundings and working conditions which are unsanitary, hazardous, or dangerous to employees; an individual who is authorized to take prompt corrective measures to eliminate these issues.

<a id="term-57"></a>

**Confined space:** A work area large enough for a person to work in, but with limited means of entry and exit and not designed for continuous occupancy. Tanks, vessels, silos, pits, vaults, and hoppers are examples of confined spaces. Also see permit-required confined space.

<a id="term-97"></a>

**Excavation:** Any man-made cut, cavity, trench, or depression in an earth surface, formed by removing earth. It can be made for anything from basements to highways. Also see trench.

<a id="term-99"></a>

**Flammable:** Capable of easily igniting and rapidly burning; used to describe a fuel with a flash point below 100°F (38°C).

<a id="term-100"></a>

**Flash burn:** The damage that can be done to eyes after even brief exposure to ultraviolet light from arc welding. A flash burn requires medical attention.

<a id="term-101"></a>

**Flash point:** The temperature at which fuel gives off enough gases (vapors) to burn.

<a id="term-119"></a>

**Ground fault circuit interrupter (GFCI):** A device that interrupts and de-energizes an electrical circuit to protect a person from electrocution. Also, a circuit breaker designed to protect people from electric shock and to protect equipment from damage by interrupting the flow of electricity if a circuit fault occurs.

<a id="term-120"></a>

**Ground fault protection:** Protection against short circuits; a safety device cuts power off as soon as it senses any imbalance between incoming and outgoing current.

<a id="term-118"></a>

**Ground fault:** An unintentional, electrically conducting connection between an ungrounded conductor of an electrical circuit and the normally noncurrent-carrying conductors, metal objects, or the earth. Also, incidental grounding of a conducting electrical wire.

<a id="term-117"></a>

**Ground:** The conducting connection between electrical equipment or an electrical circuit and the earth.

<a id="term-121"></a>

**Guarded:** Enclosed, fenced, covered, or otherwise protected by barriers, rails, covers, or platforms to prevent dangerous contact.

<a id="term-123"></a>

**Hallucinogen:** A class of drugs that distort the perception of reality and cause hallucinations.

<a id="term-127"></a>

**Hazard Communication Standard (HAZCOM):** The Occupational Safety and Health standard that requires contractors to educate employees about hazardous chemicals on the job site and how to work with them safely.

<a id="term-129"></a>

**High-efficiency particulate arrestance (HEPA) filters:** Special filters designed to capture very small particles. HEPA filters have a minimum efficiency of 99.97 percent, capturing particles that are 0.3 microns (0.0000117 inch) or larger in size.

<a id="term-135"></a>

**Incident:** According to the US Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), an unplanned event that does not result in personal injury but may result in property damage or is worthy of recording.

<a id="term-147"></a>

**Lanyard:** A cord, rope, or similar material used to limit the travel of two connected objects. Also, a short section of rope or strap, one end of which is attached to a worker's safety harness and the other to a strong anchor point above the work area.

<a id="term-158"></a>

**Lockout/tagout:** A formal procedure for taking equipment out of service and ensuring that it cannot be operated until a qualified person has removed the lock and/or warning tag.

<a id="term-164"></a>

**Maximum intended load:** The total weight of all people, equipment, tools, materials, and loads that a ladder can hold at one time.

<a id="term-167"></a>

**Methamphetamine:** A highly addictive crystalline drug, derived from amphetamines, that affects the central nervous system.

<a id="term-169"></a>

**Midrail:** Mid-level, horizontal board required on all open sides of scaffolds and platforms that are more than 14 inches (35 cm) from the face of the structure and more than 10 feet (3.05 m) above the ground. It is placed halfway between the toeboard and the top rail.

<a id="term-179"></a>

**Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA):** An agency of the US Department of Labor. Also refers to the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970, a law that applies to more than 111 million workers and 7 million job sites in the country. Also, an agency of the US Department of Labor with the mission to establish and enforce safe working conditions for the US workforce.

<a id="term-181"></a>

**Opiates:** A narcotic painkiller derived from the opium poppy plant or synthetically manufactured. Heroin is the most commonly used opiate.

<a id="term-189"></a>

**Permit-required confined space:** A confined space that has been evaluated and found to have actual or potential hazards, such as a toxic atmosphere or other serious safety or health hazard. Workers need written authorization to enter a permit-required confined space. Also see confined space.

<a id="term-188"></a>

**Permit:** A legal document that allows a task to be undertaken.

<a id="term-190"></a>

**Personal protective equipment (PPE):** Equipment or clothing designed to prevent or reduce injuries.

<a id="term-198"></a>

**Planked:** Having pieces of material 2 inches (5 cm) thick or greater and 6 inches (15 cm) wide or greater used as flooring, decking, or scaffold decks.

<a id="term-209"></a>

**Proximity work:** Work done near a hazard but not actually in contact with it.

<a id="term-211"></a>

**Qualified person:** A person who, by possession of a recognized degree, certificate, or professional standing, or by extensive knowledge, training, and experience, has demonstrated the ability to solve or prevent problems relating to a certain subject, work, or project. Also, a person who, through the possession of a recognized degree, certificate, or professional standing, or one who has gained extensive knowledge, training, and experience, has successfully demonstrated his or her ability to solve problems relating to the subject matter, the work, or the project.

<a id="term-220"></a>

**Rejection criteria:** Standards, rules, or tests on which a decision can be based to remove an object or device from service because it is no longer safe.

<a id="term-222"></a>

**Respirator:** A device that provides clean, filtered air for breathing, no matter what is in the surrounding air.

<a id="term-230"></a>

**Safety culture:** The culture created when the whole company sees the value of a safe work environment.

<a id="term-231"></a>

**Safety data sheet (SDS):** A document that must accompany any hazardous substance. The SDS identifies the substance and gives the exposure limits, the physical and chemical characteristics, the kind of hazard it presents, precautions for safe handling and use, and specific control measures.

<a id="term-232"></a>

**Scaffold:** An elevated platform for workers and materials.

<a id="term-240"></a>

**Sexual harassment:** A type of discrimination that results from unwelcome sexual advances, requests, or other verbal or physical behavior with sexual overtones.

<a id="term-244"></a>

**Shielding:** A structure used to protect workers in trenches.

<a id="term-245"></a>

**Shoring:** A support system designed to prevent a trench or excavation cave-in.

<a id="term-247"></a>

**Silicosis:** A serious lung disease resulting from the inhalation of crystalline silica particles.

<a id="term-249"></a>

**Six-foot rule:** A rule stating that platforms or work surfaces with unprotected sides or edges that are 6 feet (2 m) or higher than the ground or level below it require fall protection.

<a id="term-256"></a>

**Spoil:** Material such as earth removed while digging a trench or excavation.

<a id="term-272"></a>

**Synthetic drugs:** A drug with properties and effects similar to known substances but having a slightly altered chemical structure. Such drugs are often not illegal since they are somewhat different than well-defined restricted or illegal substances. The two typical categories are cannabinoids (lab-produced THC or marijuana substitutes) and cathinones, which are designed to mimic the effects of cocaine or methamphetamines.

<a id="term-283"></a>

**Toeboard:** A vertical barrier at floor level attached along exposed edges of a platform, runway, or ramp to prevent materials and people from falling.

<a id="term-284"></a>

**Top rail:** A top-level, horizontal board required on all open sides of scaffolds and platforms that are more than 14 inches (36 cm) from the face of the structure and more than 10 feet (3 m) above the ground.

<a id="term-286"></a>

**Trench:** A narrow excavation made below the surface of the ground that is generally deeper than it is wide, with a maximum width of 15 feet (4.6 m). Also see excavation.

<a id="term-297"></a>

**Welding curtain:** A protective screen set up around a welding operation designed to safeguard workers not directly involved in that operation.

<a id="term-299"></a>

**Whip check:** A safety attachment used to prevent whiplashing in hoses when they are inadvertently uncoupled.

<a id="term-301"></a>

**Wind sock:** A cloth cone open at both ends mounted in a high place to show which direction the wind is blowing.

<a id="term-304"></a>

**Work zone:** The area in which a forklift may come in contact with objects or people, either with the rear end or the front forks.

<a id="term-306"></a>

**Zero tolerance:** The policy of applying laws or penalties to even minor infringements of a code in order to reinforce its overall importance, typically related to drug and alcohol abuse when applied to the workplace.

## Construction Mathematics and Measurement {#topic-3}

<a id="term-4"></a>

**Acute angle:** Any angle greater than 0° and less than 90°.

<a id="term-5"></a>

**Adjacent angles:** Angles that have the same vertex and one side in common.

<a id="term-9"></a>

**Angle:** The shape made by two straight lines coming together at a point. The space between those two lines is measured in degrees.

<a id="term-17"></a>

**Area:** The amount of space contained in a two-dimensional object such as a rectangle, circle, or square.

<a id="term-20"></a>

**Base:** As it relates to triangles, the base is the line forming the bottom of the triangle.

<a id="term-22"></a>

**Bevel:** The inclination of a surface that intersects another surface at any angle less than 90 degrees; also used as a verb, meaning to cut or trim an edge to an angled profile.

<a id="term-23"></a>

**Bisect:** To divide into two equal parts. For example, when an angle is bisected, the two resulting angles are equal.

<a id="term-44"></a>

**Circles:** A closed curved line drawn at a constant distance around a central point. A circle measures 360°.

<a id="term-45"></a>

**Circumference:** The distance around the curved line that forms the circle.

<a id="term-54"></a>

**Concave:** Having a shape that curves inward, such as the interior of a circle. Convex is an antonym, meaning a shape that curves outward.

<a id="term-67"></a>

**Cube:** A three-dimensional object whose sides are all the same length.

<a id="term-69"></a>

**Decimal:** The part of a number represented by digits to the right of a point, called a decimal point. For example, in the number 1.25, .25 is the decimal portion of the number. In this case, it represents the fraction 25/100.

<a id="term-70"></a>

**Degree:** A unit of measurement for angles. For example, a right angle measures 90°.

<a id="term-71"></a>

**Denominator:** The part of a fraction below the dividing line. For example, the 2 in 1/2 is the denominator. It is equivalent to the divisor in a long division problem.

<a id="term-73"></a>

**Diagonal:** Line drawn from one corner of a rectangle or square to the farthest opposite corner.

<a id="term-74"></a>

**Diameter:** The length of a straight line that crosses from one side of a circle, through the center point, to a point on the opposite side. The diameter is the longest straight line that can be drawn inside a circle.

<a id="term-75"></a>

**Difference:** The result of subtracting one number from another. For example, in the problem 8 − 3 = 5, 5 is the difference between the two numbers.

<a id="term-76"></a>

**Digit:** Any of the numerical symbols 0 to 9.

<a id="term-79"></a>

**Dividend:** In a division problem, the number being divided is the dividend. For example, in the problem 16 ÷ 8 = 2, 16 is the dividend.

<a id="term-80"></a>

**Divisor:** In a division problem, the number that is divided into another number is called the divisor. For example, in the problem 16 ÷ 8 = 2, 8 is the divisor.

<a id="term-94"></a>

**Equation:** A mathematical statement indicating that the value of two mathematical expressions, such as 2 × 2 and 1 × 4, are equal. An equation is written using the equal sign in this manner: 2 × 2 = 1 × 4.

<a id="term-95"></a>

**Equilateral triangle:** A triangle that has three equal sides and three equal angles.

<a id="term-96"></a>

**Equivalent fractions:** Fractions having different numerators and denominators but still having equal values, such as the fractions 1/2 and 2/4.

<a id="term-106"></a>

**Force:** A push or pull on a surface. In this module, a force is the weight of an object or a fluid.

<a id="term-107"></a>

**Formula:** A mathematical process used to solve a problem. For example, the formula for finding the area of a rectangle is Side A times Side B = Area, or A × B = Area.

<a id="term-110"></a>

**Fraction:** A portion of a whole number represented by two numbers. The upper number of a fraction is called the numerator, while the bottom number is called the denominator.

<a id="term-113"></a>

**Graph:** Information shown as a picture or chart. Graphs may be represented in various forms, including line graphs and bar charts.

<a id="term-134"></a>

**Improper fraction:** A fraction whose numerator is larger than its denominator. For example, 8/4 and 6/3 are improper fractions.

<a id="term-141"></a>

**Isosceles triangle:** A triangle that has two equal sides and two equal angles.

<a id="term-161"></a>

**Mass:** The quantity of matter present.

<a id="term-168"></a>

**Metric scale:** A straightedge measuring device divided into centimeters, with each centimeter divided into 10 millimeters. Usually used for architectural drawings and sometimes referred to as a metric architect's scale.

<a id="term-172"></a>

**Mixed number:** A combination of a whole number with a fraction or decimal. Examples of mixed numbers are 3 7/16, 5.75, and 1 1/4.

<a id="term-174"></a>

**Negative numbers:** Numbers less than zero. For example, −1, −2, and −3 are negative numbers.

<a id="term-177"></a>

**Numerator:** The part of a fraction above the dividing line. For example, the 1 in 1/2 is the numerator. It is equivalent to the dividend in a long division problem.

<a id="term-178"></a>

**Obtuse angle:** Any angle greater than 90° and less than 180°.

<a id="term-182"></a>

**Opposite angles:** Two angles that are formed by two straight lines crossing. They are always equal.

<a id="term-187"></a>

**Perimeter:** The distance around the outside of a closed shape, such as a rectangle, circle, square, or any irregular shape.

<a id="term-191"></a>

**Pi:** A mathematical constant value approximately equal to 3.14 (or 22/7) used to determine the area and circumference of circles. It is sometimes symbolized by the Greek letter π.

<a id="term-195"></a>

**Place value:** The value that a digit represents in a whole number, determined by its place within the whole number or by its position relative to the decimal point. In the number 124, the number 2 represents 20, since it is in the tens position.

<a id="term-197"></a>

**Plane geometry:** The mathematical study of two-dimensional (flat) shapes.

<a id="term-196"></a>

**Plane:** A surface in which a straight line joining two points lies wholly within that surface.

<a id="term-203"></a>

**Positive numbers:** Numbers greater than zero. For example, 1, 2, and 3 are positive numbers. Except for zero, any number without a negative (-) sign in front of it is treated as a positive number.

<a id="term-205"></a>

**Product:** The result of a multiplication problem. For example, the product of 6 × 6 is 36.

<a id="term-212"></a>

**Quotient:** The result of a division problem. For example, when dividing 6 by 2, the quotient is 3.

<a id="term-213"></a>

**Radius:** The distance from a circle's center point to any point on the curved line, or half the width (diameter) of the circle.

<a id="term-217"></a>

**Rectangle:** A four-sided shape with four 90° angles. Opposite sides of a rectangle are always parallel and the same length. Adjacent sides are perpendicular and may or may not be equal in length.

<a id="term-221"></a>

**Remainder:** The amount left over in a division problem. For example, in the problem 34 ÷ 8, 8 goes into 34 four times (8 × 4 = 32) with 2 left over, so the remainder is 2.

<a id="term-225"></a>

**Right angle:** An angle that measures 90°. The two lines that form a right angle are perpendicular to each other. This is the angle most used in the crafts.

<a id="term-226"></a>

**Right triangle:** A triangle that includes one 90° angle.

<a id="term-233"></a>

**Scale:** The ratio between the size of a drawing of an object and the size of the actual object.

<a id="term-234"></a>

**Scalene triangle:** A triangle with sides of unequal lengths.

<a id="term-237"></a>

**Sector:** A category of construction distinguished by specific types of construction, materials, equipment, and skills. The four sectors of construction are residential, commercial, industrial, and heavy civil/infrastructure.

<a id="term-253"></a>

**Solid geometry:** The mathematical study of three-dimensional shapes.

<a id="term-258"></a>

**Square:** (1) A special type of rectangle with four equal sides and four 90° angles. (2) The product of a number multiplied by itself. For example, 25 is the square of 5; 16 is the square of 4. Also, used as an adjective in this context, to have the shape of a square where two components are at precise 90-degree angles (perpendicular) to each other.

<a id="term-263"></a>

**Straight angle:** A 180° angle or flat line.

<a id="term-269"></a>

**Sum:** The result of an addition problem. For example, in the problem 7 + 8 = 15, 15 is the sum.

<a id="term-285"></a>

**Torque:** The rotating or twisting force applied to an object such as a fastener or the shaft of a motor. Also, the turning force produced by the drill. As the bit's turning speed increases, torque decreases, and vice versa.

<a id="term-287"></a>

**Triangle:** A closed shape that has three sides and three angles.

<a id="term-289"></a>

**Unit:** A standardized quantity used for a particular kind of measurement.

<a id="term-291"></a>

**Vertex:** The point at which two or more lines or curves come together.

<a id="term-294"></a>

**Volume:** The amount of space contained within a three-dimensional space.

<a id="term-300"></a>

**Whole numbers:** Complete number units without fractions or decimals.

## Hand Tools, Power Tools and Fabrication {#topic-4}

<a id="term-0"></a>

**Abrasive:** A substance, such as sandpaper, that is used to wear away material.

<a id="term-6"></a>

**Alternating current (AC):** The common power supplied to most all wired devices, where the current reverses its direction many times per second. AC power is the type of power generated and distributed throughout settled areas.

<a id="term-8"></a>

**Amp hour (Ah):** A rating that describes the maximum amps a battery can provide continuously for up to 60 minutes. Drawing more amps than the battery's rating is possible, but it reduces the amount of time before the battery is drained.

<a id="term-12"></a>

**Arbor:** The end of a circular saw shaft where the blade is mounted.

<a id="term-16"></a>

**Arc welding:** The joining of metal parts by fusion, in which the necessary heat is produced by means of an electric arc.

<a id="term-18"></a>

**Auger bit:** A drill bit with a spiral cutting edge for boring holes in wood and other materials.

<a id="term-28"></a>

**Brazing:** A process using heat in excess of 800°F (427°C) to melt a filler metal that is drawn into a connection. Brazing is commonly used to join copper pipe.

<a id="term-36"></a>

**Carbide:** A very hard material made of tungsten carbide and heavy metals such as cobalt. Commonly baked on to the tips of masonry bits and to the edges of saw blades.

<a id="term-39"></a>

**Charge cycle:** Describes one complete charge and discharge cycle for a rechargeable battery.

<a id="term-40"></a>

**Cheater bar:** A length of pipe or rod used to increase the leverage on a wrench or similar tool. The increased leverage provided by a cheater bar often exceeds the tool's capacity and strength, resulting in a damaged tool as well as damaged workpieces.

<a id="term-43"></a>

**Chuck key:** A small, T-shaped steel piece used to open and close the chuck on power drills. Cordless drills typically do not have a chuck key.

<a id="term-42"></a>

**Chuck:** A clamping device that holds an attachment; for example, the chuck of the drill holds the drill bit.

<a id="term-61"></a>

**Core:** Center support member of a wire rope around which the strands are laid.

<a id="term-62"></a>

**Countersink:** A bit or drill used to set the head of a screw at or below the surface of the material.

<a id="term-78"></a>

**Direct current (DC):** An electric power supply where the current flows in one direction only. DC power is supplied by batteries and by transformer-rectifiers that change AC power to DC.

<a id="term-81"></a>

**Dowel:** A pin or rod, usually round, that fits into a corresponding hole to fasten or align two workpieces.

<a id="term-91"></a>

**Emery cloth:** A material made with a thin cloth backing, coated with an abrasive that is tightly bonded to the backing; typically sold in sheets or narrow rolls.

<a id="term-102"></a>

**Flats:** The flat, straight sides of a wrench opening; also, the sides on a nut or bolt head.

<a id="term-104"></a>

**Flush:** As an adjective, to be even or at the same level with another object. For example, when a nail is fully seated, the top of the head is flush (even) with, or slightly beyond, the surface of the wood.

<a id="term-108"></a>

**Forstner bit:** A bit designed for use in wood or similar soft material. The design allows it to drill a flat-bottom blind hole in material.

<a id="term-116"></a>

**Grit:** A granular, sand-like material used to make sandpaper and similar materials abrasive. Grit is graded according to its texture. The grit number indicates the number of abrasive granules in a standard size (per in or per cm). The higher the grit number, the more particles in a given area, indicating a finer abrasive material.

<a id="term-133"></a>

**Hydraulic:** Powered by fluid under pressure.

<a id="term-144"></a>

**Joint:** The intersection of workpiece components where a physical connection is generally made. Joints are often named by the style of connection, such as butt joints and miter joints.

<a id="term-146"></a>

**Kerf:** The channel created by a saw blade passing through the material, which is equal to the width of the blade teeth.

<a id="term-152"></a>

**Level:** Used as an adjective in this context, meaning flat or even with another surface or in relation to the Earth's horizon.

<a id="term-160"></a>

**Masonry bit:** A drill bit with a carbide tip designed to penetrate materials such as stone, brick, or concrete.

<a id="term-171"></a>

**Miter joints:** Joints made by fastening parts together with the ends cut at a similar angle, usually 90 degrees.

<a id="term-173"></a>

**Mushroomed:** Refers to the head of a tool that is struck or used for striking when the head becomes wider and distorted, like the head of a mushroom.

<a id="term-183"></a>

**Overstriking:** When a hammer misses the head of a nail, with the head going past it and allowing the hammer handle to impact the nail head instead. Overstriking is a common cause of hammer damage.

<a id="term-186"></a>

**Peening:** The process of bending, shaping, or cutting metal by striking it with a tool.

<a id="term-199"></a>

**Plumb:** Perfectly vertical, meaning a surface is at a right angle (perpendicular) to the horizon or other surface used for comparison.

<a id="term-202"></a>

**Pneumatic:** Powered by air pressure, such as a pneumatic tool.

<a id="term-216"></a>

**Reciprocating:** Moving backward and forward on a straight line.

<a id="term-223"></a>

**Revolutions per minute (rpm):** The rotational speed of a motor or shaft, based on the number of times it rotates each minute.

<a id="term-238"></a>

**Self-feed bit:** A bit that uses a lead screw to pull the larger rear portion of the bit into the wood, making it simple to drill larger holes.

<a id="term-242"></a>

**Shank:** The smooth part of a drill bit that fits into the chuck.

<a id="term-262"></a>

**Step drill bit:** A bit that includes steps that enable the user to cut holes in metal to a desired diameter.

<a id="term-277"></a>

**Tang:** Tapered, pointed end of a file designed for insertion into a file handle.

<a id="term-280"></a>

**Tempered:** Treated with heat to enhance or restore steel hardness.

<a id="term-281"></a>

**Threaded shank:** A connecting end of a fastener, such as a bolt, with a series of spiral grooves cut into it. The grooves are designed to mate with grooves cut into another object in order to join them together.

<a id="term-288"></a>

**Trigger lock:** A small lever, switch, or part that can be used to activate a locking catch or spring to hold a power tool trigger in the operating mode without finger pressure.

<a id="term-293"></a>

**Voltage rating:** A measurement of the power the tool can deliver.

<a id="term-296"></a>

**Welding:** The process of heating two or more metal workpieces to their liquid state and fusing them to create a very strong joint.

## Construction Drawings {#topic-5}

<a id="term-14"></a>

**Architect's scale:** A specialized ruler used in making or measuring reduced scale drawings. The ruler is marked with a range of calibrated ratios for laying out distances, with scales indicating feet, inches, and fractions of inches. Used on drawings other than site plans.

<a id="term-15"></a>

**Architectural plans:** Drawings that show the design of the project. Also called architectural drawings.

<a id="term-25"></a>

**Blueprints:** A traditional method of reproducing technical drawings using a contact print process and light-sensitive paper that resulted in white lines on a blue background. Also, the traditional name used to describe construction drawings.

<a id="term-30"></a>

**Building information modeling (BIM):** A process in which software reads computer files containing building data to create a virtual picture of a facility before it is built. BIM objects represent many dimensions of construction, and allow builders to adjust the plans prior to beginning actual construction.

<a id="term-38"></a>

**Change order:** A written order by the owner of a project for the contractor to make a change in time, amount, or specifications.

<a id="term-46"></a>

**Civil plans:** Drawings that show the location of the building on the site from an aerial view, including contours, trees, construction features, and dimensions.

<a id="term-52"></a>

**Computer-aided design (CAD):** The use of computers to digitally sketch construction designs, allowing other people to see how aspects of a structure would look and work together when built.

<a id="term-53"></a>

**Computer-aided drafting (CAD):** The making of a set of construction drawings with the aid of a computer.

<a id="term-59"></a>

**Contour lines:** Solid or dashed lines showing the elevation of the earth on a civil drawing.

<a id="term-72"></a>

**Detail drawings:** Enlarged views of part of a drawing used to show an area more clearly.

<a id="term-77"></a>

**Dimension line:** A line on a drawing with a measurement indicating length.

<a id="term-82"></a>

**Drawing revision control:** A system for recording, documenting, and identifying drawing revisions by using a numeric or alphabetic sequence.

<a id="term-87"></a>

**Electrical plans:** Engineered drawings that show all electrical supply and distribution.

<a id="term-89"></a>

**Elevation (EL):** Height above sea level, or other defined surface, usually expressed in feet or meters.

<a id="term-90"></a>

**Elevation drawing:** Side view of a building or object, showing height and width.

<a id="term-93"></a>

**Engineer's scale:** A straightedge measuring device divided uniformly into multiples of 10 divisions per inch so that drawings can be made with decimal values. Used mainly for land measurements on site plans.

<a id="term-98"></a>

**Fire protection plan:** A drawing that shows the details of the building's sprinkler system.

<a id="term-103"></a>

**Floor plan:** A drawing that provides an aerial view of the layout of each room.

<a id="term-109"></a>

**Foundation plan:** A drawing that shows the layout and elevation of the building foundation.

<a id="term-128"></a>

**Hidden line:** A dashed line showing an object obstructed from view by another object.

<a id="term-140"></a>

**Invert:** To reverse the order or position of numbers. In fractions, inverting means reversing the positions of the numerator and denominator, so 3/4 becomes 4/3. When dividing by fractions, one fraction is inverted.

<a id="term-149"></a>

**Leader:** In drafting, the line on which an arrowhead is placed and used to identify a component.

<a id="term-151"></a>

**Legend:** A description of the symbols and abbreviations used in a set of drawings.

<a id="term-165"></a>

**Mechanical plans:** Engineered drawings that show the mechanical systems, such as motors and piping.

<a id="term-176"></a>

**Not to scale (NTS):** Describes drawings that show relative positions and sizes only, without scale.

<a id="term-194"></a>

**Piping and instrumentation drawings (P&IDs):** Schematic diagrams of a complete piping system.

<a id="term-200"></a>

**Plumbing isometric drawing:** A type of three-dimensional drawing that depicts a plumbing system.

<a id="term-201"></a>

**Plumbing plans:** Engineered drawings that show the layout for the plumbing system.

<a id="term-228"></a>

**Roof plan:** A drawing of the view of the roof from above the building.

<a id="term-235"></a>

**Schematic:** A one-line drawing showing the flow path for electrical circuitry or the relationship of all parts of a system.

<a id="term-236"></a>

**Section drawing:** A cross-sectional view of a specific location, showing the inside of an object or building.

<a id="term-254"></a>

**Specifications:** Precise written presentation of the details of a plan.

<a id="term-266"></a>

**Structural plans:** A set of engineered drawings used to support the architectural design.

<a id="term-271"></a>

**Symbols:** Drawings that represent a material or component on a plan.

<a id="term-282"></a>

**Title block:** A part of a drawing sheet that includes some general information about the project.

## Rigging and Material Handling {#topic-6}

<a id="term-24"></a>

**Block and tackle:** A simple rope-and-pulley system used to lift loads.

<a id="term-27"></a>

**Bowline:** A knot used to form a loop that neither slips nor jams; sometimes referred to as a rescue knot or the king of knots.

<a id="term-29"></a>

**Bridle:** A configuration using two or more slings to connect a load to a single hoist hook.

<a id="term-32"></a>

**Bull ring:** A single ring used to attach multiple slings to a hoist hook.

<a id="term-35"></a>

**Capsize:** To change the form and rearrange the parts of a knot, usually by pulling on specific ends of the knot.

<a id="term-48"></a>

**Clove hitch:** A knot that consists of two half hitches made in opposite directions; used to temporarily secure a rope to an object.

<a id="term-55"></a>

**Concrete mule:** A wheeled device used when a concrete pour is in a location that a concrete delivery truck or pump cannot reach; sometimes referred to as a Georgia buggy.

<a id="term-68"></a>

**Cylinder cart:** A two-wheeled cart that is used to transport cylinders, or bottles, of compressed gases.

<a id="term-83"></a>

**Drum cart:** A wheeled cart that is used to transport heavier-weight 55-gallon drums/barrels.

<a id="term-84"></a>

**Drum dolly:** A wheeled circular platform or a caddy with a handle that is used to transport lighter-weight 55-gallon drums/barrels.

<a id="term-111"></a>

**Freight elevator:** An elevator used to transport materials from floor to floor.

<a id="term-122"></a>

**Half hitch:** A knot tied by passing the working end of a rope around an object, across the standing part of the rope, and then through the resulting loop; often used as an element in forming other knots or added to make other knots more secure.

<a id="term-124"></a>

**Hand line:** A line attached to a tool or object so a worker can pull it up after climbing a ladder or scaffold.

<a id="term-125"></a>

**Hand truck:** Two-wheeled cart that is used to transport large, heavy loads; also known as a dolly.

<a id="term-130"></a>

**Hitch:** The rigging configuration by which a sling connects the load to the hoist hook. The three basic types of hitches are vertical, choker, and basket.

<a id="term-131"></a>

**Hoist:** A device that applies a mechanical force for lifting or lowering a load.

<a id="term-137"></a>

**Industrial forklift:** A vehicle with a power-operated pronged platform that can be raised and lowered for insertion under a load to be lifted and moved.

<a id="term-153"></a>

**Lifting clamp:** A device used to move loads such as steel plates or concrete panels without the use of slings.

<a id="term-155"></a>

**Load control:** The safe and efficient practice of load manipulation, using proper communication and handling techniques.

<a id="term-156"></a>

**Load stress:** The strain or tension applied on the rigging by the weight of the suspended load.

<a id="term-154"></a>

**Load:** The total amount of what is being lifted, including all slings, hitches, and hardware.

<a id="term-162"></a>

**Master link:** The main connection fitting for chain slings.

<a id="term-163"></a>

**Material cart:** Four-wheeled device used to transport materials around a job site.

<a id="term-180"></a>

**One-rope lay:** The lengthwise distance it takes for one strand of a wire rope to make one complete turn around the core.

<a id="term-184"></a>

**Pallet jack:** A device used to lift and move heavy or stacked pallets; also known as a pallet truck.

<a id="term-192"></a>

**Pipe mule:** A two-wheeled device used to transport medium-length pieces of pipe, tubing, or scaffolding; sometimes referred to as a tunnel buggy.

<a id="term-193"></a>

**Pipe transport:** A wheeled device similar to a pipe mule, but used to move larger pieces of pipe.

<a id="term-204"></a>

**Powered wheelbarrow:** A vehicle similar to a manual wheelbarrow, but powered by an electric or gas motor; also known as a power buggy.

<a id="term-215"></a>

**Rated capacity:** The maximum load weight a sling or piece of hardware or equipment can hold or lift; also referred to as the working load limit (WLL).

<a id="term-224"></a>

**Rigging hook:** An item of rigging hardware used to attach a sling to a load.

<a id="term-227"></a>

**Roller skids:** A device that includes a surface table and two, three, or four roller skids. Materials that are to be moved are placed on the table surface and then pushed on the skids.

<a id="term-229"></a>

**Rough terrain forklift:** Similar to an industrial forklift, but designed to be used on rough surfaces. Rough terrain forklifts are characterized by large pneumatic tires, usually with deep treads that allow the vehicle to grab onto the roughest of roads or ground cover without sliding or slipping.

<a id="term-241"></a>

**Shackle:** Coupling device used in an appropriate lifting apparatus to connect the rope to eye fittings, hooks, or other connectors.

<a id="term-243"></a>

**Sheave:** A grooved pulley-wheel for changing the direction of a rope's pull; often found on a crane.

<a id="term-246"></a>

**Signaler:** A person who is responsible for directing a vehicle when the driver's vision is blocked in any way.

<a id="term-251"></a>

**Sling legs:** The parts of the sling that reach from the attachment device around the object being lifted.

<a id="term-252"></a>

**Sling reach:** A measure taken from the master link of the sling, where it bears weight, to either the end fitting of the sling or the lowest point on the basket.

<a id="term-250"></a>

**Sling:** Wire rope, alloy steel chain, metal mesh fabric, synthetic rope, synthetic webbing, or jacketed synthetic continuous loop fibers made into forms, with or without end fittings, used to handle loads.

<a id="term-255"></a>

**Splice:** To join together.

<a id="term-257"></a>

**Spotter:** A person who walks in front of another worker who is carrying or transporting a long load to ensure there is a clear, unobstructed path.

<a id="term-259"></a>

**Square knot:** A knot made of two reverse half-knots and typically used to join the ends of two ropes of similar diameters; also called a reef knot.

<a id="term-260"></a>

**Standing end:** The end of a rope that is not being knotted.

<a id="term-261"></a>

**Standing part:** The portion of a rope that is between the standing end and the working end.

<a id="term-264"></a>

**Strand:** A group of wires wound, or laid, around a center wire, or core. Strands are laid around a supporting core to form a rope.

<a id="term-265"></a>

**Stripping:** Damaging or distorting the head or threads of a fastener.

<a id="term-276"></a>

**Tag line:** Rope that runs from the load to the ground. Riggers hold on to tag lines to keep a load from swinging or spinning during the lift.

<a id="term-279"></a>

**Tattle-tail:** Cord attached to the strands of an endless loop sling. It protrudes from the jacket. A tattle-tail is used to determine if an endless sling has been stretched or overloaded.

<a id="term-290"></a>

**Unstranding:** Describes wire rope strands that have become untwisted. This weakens the rope and makes it easier to break.

<a id="term-295"></a>

**Warning yarn:** A component of the sling that shows the rigger whether the sling has suffered too much damage to be used.

<a id="term-298"></a>

**Wheelbarrow:** A one- or two-wheeled vehicle with handles at the rear that is used to carry small loads.

<a id="term-302"></a>

**Wire rope:** A rope made from steel wires that are formed into strands and then laid around a supporting core to form a complete rope; sometimes called cable.

<a id="term-305"></a>

**Working end:** The end of a rope that is being used to tie a knot.

## Communication and Employability {#topic-7}

<a id="term-1"></a>

**Absenteeism:** A consistent failure to show up for work.

<a id="term-3"></a>

**Active listening:** A process that involves respecting others, listening to what is being said, and understanding what is being said.

<a id="term-10"></a>

**Appendix:** A source of detailed or specific information placed at the end of a section, a chapter, or a book.

<a id="term-26"></a>

**Body language:** A person's facial expression, physical posture, gestures, and use of space, which communicate feelings and ideas.

<a id="term-31"></a>

**Bullets:** Large, vertically aligned dots that highlight items in a list.

<a id="term-33"></a>

**Bullying:** Unwanted, aggressive behavior that involves a real or perceived power imbalance. This form of harassment may include offensive, persistent, insulting, or physically threatening behavior directed at an individual.

<a id="term-51"></a>

**Compromise:** When people involved in a disagreement make concessions to reach a solution that everyone agrees on.

<a id="term-56"></a>

**Confidentiality:** Privacy of information.

<a id="term-58"></a>

**Constructive criticism:** A positive offer of advice intended to help someone correct mistakes or improve actions.

<a id="term-86"></a>

**Earn as You Learn:** An approach to education that pays trainees wages for work completed as part of an on-the-job learning (OJL) program.

<a id="term-88"></a>

**Electronic signature:** A signature that is used to sign electronic documents by capturing handwritten signatures through computer technology and attaching them to the document or file.

<a id="term-105"></a>

**Font:** The type style used for letters and numbers.

<a id="term-112"></a>

**Glossary:** An alphabetical list of terms and definitions.

<a id="term-126"></a>

**Harassment:** A type of discrimination that can be based on race, age, disabilities, sex, religion, cultural issues, health, or language barriers.

<a id="term-136"></a>

**Index:** An alphabetical list of topics, along with the page numbers where each topic appears.

<a id="term-139"></a>

**Initiative:** The ability to work without constant supervision and solve problems independently.

<a id="term-142"></a>

**Italics:** Letters and numbers that lean to the right rather than stand straight up.

<a id="term-143"></a>

**Jargon:** Specialized terms used in a specific industry.

<a id="term-150"></a>

**Leadership:** The ability to set an example for others to follow by exercising authority and responsibility.

<a id="term-159"></a>

**Management system:** The organization of a company's management, including reporting procedures, supervisory responsibility, and administration.

<a id="term-166"></a>

<a id="term-170"></a>

**Mission statement:** A statement of how a company does business.

<a id="term-175"></a>

**Nonverbal communication:** All communication that does not use words. This includes tone of voice, appearance, personal environment, use of time, and body language.

<a id="term-185"></a>

<a id="term-206"></a>

**Professionalism:** Integrity and work-appropriate manners.

<a id="term-218"></a>

<a id="term-239"></a>

<a id="term-274"></a>

<a id="term-273"></a>

**Table:** A way to present important text and numbers so they can be read and understood at a glance.

<a id="term-275"></a>

**Tactful:** Being aware of the effects of your statements and actions on others.

<a id="term-278"></a>

**Tardiness:** Habitually showing up late for work.

<a id="term-303"></a>

**Work ethic:** Work habits that are the foundation of a person's ability to do his or her job.
