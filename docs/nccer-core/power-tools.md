# Module 00104: Introduction to Power Tools

This page organizes Module 00104 into 47 active-recall cards. Answer each **Front** question before expanding the **Back** to check your response.

:::danger[Safety boundary]
These study cards do not replace employer procedures, manufacturer instructions, equipment ratings, hands-on training, or qualified supervision. Use a power tool only when you are trained and authorized, and follow the current job-site rules.
:::

:::info[How to review]
Mark a card **Know** only when you can name the correct tool, its main hazard, the pre-use check, and the safe action without looking. Repeat **Unsure** and **Do not know** cards first, then practice the real task under qualified supervision.
:::

:::caution[Course scope]
These cards summarize the supplied NCCER Core Module 00104 material. Module 00101, Basic Safety, should be completed first. Current employer rules and manufacturer instructions control whenever they are more specific.
:::

## Section 1: Power Drills/Drivers

### 1.0.0 Power Drills/Drivers

**Front:** What four power-tool categories does this module introduce, and what powers each one?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Electric tools use AC from a cord or DC from a battery; pneumatic tools use compressed air; specialty tools include powder-actuated fastening tools; and hydraulic tools use pressurized fluid. Specialty and hydraulic tools require task-specific training and strict procedure control.

</details>

### 1.1.0 Basic Power Tool Safety Guidelines

**Front:** What general rules apply before and during the use of any power tool?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Read the operating instructions, receive proper instruction and supervision, select the correct tool, inspect the tool and its cord or hose, wear required PPE, secure clothing and hair, control the work area, and stay focused. Disconnect the energy source before maintenance or changing parts; never carry a tool by its cord or hose or lock its trigger on.

</details>

### 1.2.1 Power Drills and Bits

**Front:** How do you match common drill bits to materials and tasks?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Twist bits drill wood, plastic, or metal at the appropriate speed; Forstner bits make flat-bottom holes in wood; spade, auger, and self-feed bits bore wood; carbide-tipped masonry bits drill masonry; and step bits enlarge holes in thin metal. Use only a bit type, size, shank, and speed suitable for the drill and material.

</details>

### 1.2.2 Cordless Tools and Batteries

**Front:** What do charge-cycle, amp-hour, and voltage ratings tell you about a cordless-tool battery?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A charge cycle is one complete charge and discharge. Amp-hours describe how much current a battery can supply over time, so a higher current draw shortens runtime. Voltage indicates the tool's available power. Use only the charger and battery system approved by the manufacturer.

</details>

### 1.2.3 Power Drill Safety and Maintenance Guidelines

**Front:** What are the most important drill-specific safety checks?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Wear eye protection, choose a sharp correct bit, secure it in the chuck, keep hands away from the bit and chuck, and use a stable two-handed grip. Verify hidden wiring, piping, insulation, and hazards before drilling through a surface. Use required grounding or GFCI protection and never use a damaged cord, plug, ground prong, or tool.

</details>

### 1.2.4 Preparing to Use a Power Drill

**Front:** How should a power drill be prepared before making a hole?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Put on required PPE, mark the hole, select the correct bit and speed, disconnect the power source, install and securely tighten the bit, remove the chuck key, and reconnect power only after confirming the trigger is off. Clamp small workpieces and verify the area behind the drilling surface.

</details>

### 1.2.5 Operating Power Drills

**Front:** What technique keeps a drill controlled and protects the bit and workpiece?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Hold the drill square to the work with both hands, begin slowly so the bit does not wander, then use the correct speed and steady pressure. Do not force or ram the drill. Back the bit out as needed to clear chips, stop if it binds, and release the trigger before setting the tool down.

</details>

### 1.2.6 Electromagnetic Drill Presses

**Front:** What is an electromagnetic drill press used for, and what is its critical limitation?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A magnetic drill press is a portable machine for drilling thick ferrous metal while an electromagnet holds its base to the work. The magnet requires a clean, flat, sufficiently thick ferrous surface and continuous power. Secure the machine with the required safety chain or restraint because power loss releases the magnet.

</details>

### 1.3.0 Hammer Drills and Rotary Hammers

**Front:** How do hammer drills differ from rotary hammers?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Both rotate and hammer a masonry bit, but rotary hammers turn more slowly and strike harder for heavier work. Rotary hammers commonly use SDS-type slotted shanks and special keyless chucks that permit axial bit movement; the bit must match the chuck system.

</details>

### 1.3.1 Hammer Drill Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** What extra precautions apply to hammer drills and rotary hammers?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Wear eye and hearing protection and follow silica controls when dust can be produced. Use both hands, secure small work, keep sparks away from flammables, inspect the tool and masonry-rated bit, and disconnect power before changing bits or adjustments.

</details>

### 1.3.2 Operating Hammer Drills

**Front:** What is the safe operating sequence for starting a hole with a hammer drill?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Select and install the correct bit with power disconnected, mark the location, choose the required hammer and speed settings, and start slowly on the mark while maintaining controlled pressure. Establish a shallow guide hole, increase to the required speed, complete the hole without forcing the tool, and clean the hole using the approved dust-control method.

</details>

### 1.3.3 Impact Drivers

**Front:** When is an impact driver preferable to a drill, and when is it not?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** An impact driver applies high rotational torque through a hex collet and is effective for repeatedly driving or loosening screws, nuts, and lag bolts, especially in hard material. Its force and limited speed control make it less suitable for precision drilling or fragile material.

</details>

### 1.3.4 Impact Driver Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** What must be checked before using an impact driver on a fastener?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Wear required PPE, inspect and clean the tool, use an impact-rated bit or socket that fits, and confirm that the fastener and material can withstand the torque. Excess torque can strip fasteners, split wood, break brittle material, or twist the tool out of control.

</details>

### 1.3.5 Operating Impact Drivers

**Front:** How do you install a bit and control an impact driver while fastening?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Disconnect power, pull the collet forward, insert the correct bit, and verify the collet locks it. Reconnect power, set direction, seat the bit or socket squarely on the fastener, start slowly, maintain control, and release the trigger as soon as the fastener reaches the required position or torque.

</details>

### 1.4.1 Pneumatic Drills

**Front:** What distinguishes a pneumatic drill from an electric drill?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A pneumatic drill uses compressed air through a hose rather than an electric motor, so it can provide high power for its weight and work where electricity is unsuitable or unavailable. Chuck size identifies the largest bit shank it can hold, not its full drilling capacity.

</details>

### 1.4.2 Impact Wrenches

**Front:** What are impact wrenches designed to do, and how do pneumatic and cordless models compare?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Impact wrenches fasten, tighten, and loosen nuts and bolts with high torque. Pneumatic models are commonly stronger but require a compressor, hose, clean dry air, and maintenance; cordless models are more portable and increasingly powerful. Both require impact-rated sockets and the correct torque specification.

</details>

### 1.4.3 Pneumatic Tool Safety and Maintenance Guidelines

**Front:** What controls make a pneumatic drill or impact wrench safe to connect and use?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Inspect the hose and fittings, verify clean dry air at the correct pressure, provide required lubrication, and install a whip check where required. Disconnect the tool from the air supply before maintenance or changing accessories, use only rated bits or impact sockets, secure the work, wear eye, hand, and hearing protection, and never use tool-pressure air for cleaning people or clothing.

</details>

### 1.4.4 Operating Pneumatic Drills and Impact Wrenches

**Front:** What sequence prevents hose and torque hazards when operating a pneumatic tool?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Put on PPE, check the oiler if required, install the proper bit or impact socket, secure the hose connection and whip check, and set the regulator to the specified pressure. Check required fastener torque, grip the tool firmly before starting, use a backup wrench when needed, and disconnect the air hose when finished.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
Never use a **trigger lock** to hold a tool on. A drill's **reversing switch** helps back out a stuck bit. Rotary hammers use **specially designed chucks**, and a pneumatic **impact wrench** is intended for tightening and loosening nuts and bolts.
:::

## Section 2: Power Saws

### 2.1.0 Circular Saws and Blades

**Front:** How do circular-saw blade types match the direction and material of a cut?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Rip blades cut with wood grain, crosscut blades cut across it, and combination blades do both. Nail-cutting and nonferrous-metal blades serve specific materials. Blade diameter, arbor size, material rating, tooth direction, and maximum rpm must all match the saw and task; metric and fractional systems are not interchangeable.

</details>

### 2.1.1 Circular Saw Safety Guidelines and Maintenance

**Front:** Which checks reduce circular-saw kickback and blade-contact hazards?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Disconnect power before adjustment, inspect the blade and guards, confirm the lower guard closes freely, set the proper depth, and support and clamp the work so the kerf cannot pinch the blade. Keep both hands on the saw, stay out of the blade path, never defeat a guard, and keep blades clean and sharp.

</details>

### 2.1.2 Operating Circular Saws

**Front:** How should a circular saw enter and travel through a cut?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Mark and support the work, set blade depth with power disconnected, position the baseplate with the blade clear of the material, grip both handles, and let the blade reach full speed. Push forward steadily along the cut line without forcing or twisting; release the trigger after the cut and wait for the blade to stop before setting the saw down.

</details>

### 2.2.1 Jigsaws

**Front:** What work is a jigsaw best suited for?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A jigsaw uses a fine reciprocating blade for delicate straight or curved cuts, patterns, and irregular openings in wood, thin metal, plastic, wallboard, and similar material. Use low speed for harder materials and high speed for softer materials, following blade instructions.

</details>

### 2.2.2 Reciprocating Saws

**Front:** How does a reciprocating saw differ from a jigsaw, and how is speed selected?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A reciprocating saw has a longer, tougher blade and is intended for heavier cutting and demolition, while a jigsaw favors controlled, intricate work. Use lower speed and a fine high-tooth-count blade for metal; use higher speed and the correct blade for wood and other softer material.

</details>

### 2.2.3 Jigsaw and Reciprocating Saw Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** What hazards must be checked before cutting a wall or workpiece with a reciprocating blade?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Determine what is behind or inside the material, including wiring, piping, and people. Disconnect power before installing the correct undamaged blade, clamp the work, keep hands out of the cut path, allow clearance for the full blade stroke, and never remove a moving blade from the work.

</details>

### 2.2.4 Operating Jigsaws and Reciprocating Saws

**Front:** What operating practices control vibration and blade breakage on jigsaws and reciprocating saws?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Clamp the work, select the blade and speed, hold the base or shoe firmly against the surface, start with the blade clear, and guide without side pressure or forcing. Use both hands on a reciprocating saw and keep its baseplate against the work; stop the blade completely before withdrawing or repositioning it.

</details>

### 2.3.0 Portable Band Saws

**Front:** What jobs suit a portable band saw, and how does its blade move?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A portable band saw is commonly used to cut pipe, bar, structural metal, plastic, wood, and irregular shapes, especially metal on job sites. Its continuous blade travels in one direction around guides rather than reciprocating or spinning on an arbor.

</details>

### 2.3.1 Band Saw Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** What must be inspected and controlled before using a portable band saw?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Inspect blade condition, tension, tracking, guides, guard, and power source; select the correct blade and secure the work. Keep both hands on the tool and the stop firmly against the material, keep body parts out of the blade path, and disconnect power before blade changes or adjustments.

</details>

### 2.3.2 Operating a Portable Band Saw

**Front:** How should a portable band saw be started and guided through material?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Clamp the material, place the saw's stop against the work with the blade clear, grip both handles, and let the blade reach operating speed. Lower or guide the blade steadily without forcing it, support the cutoff piece, finish the cut under control, and shut the saw off before moving away.

</details>

### 2.4.1 Power Miter Saws

**Front:** What cuts can standard, compound, and sliding compound miter saws make?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A standard miter saw pivots horizontally for straight and miter cuts. A compound saw also tilts the blade for bevel and compound cuts. A sliding compound saw adds rails that move the blade assembly forward and backward so it can cut wider stock.

</details>

### 2.4.2 Abrasive Cutoff Saws

**Front:** What is an abrasive cutoff saw used for, and why is wheel rating critical?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A cutoff or chop saw uses a high-speed abrasive wheel to make straight or angled cuts in metal such as angle iron, flat bar, and channel. Friction burns through the material, producing heat and sparks; the wheel must fit the saw and have a maximum rpm rating equal to or greater than the saw's speed.

</details>

### 2.4.3 Miter and Cutoff Saw Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** What precautions apply to both miter and abrasive cutoff saws?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Wear required PPE, use dust or silica controls where applicable, inspect the correct blade or wheel and guard, disconnect power for changes and adjustments, clamp the work, support long stock, and keep hands out of the cutting zone. Keep sparks away from people and combustibles and never use a damaged or underrated wheel.

</details>

### 2.4.4 Operating Miter and Cutoff Saws

**Front:** What is the controlled sequence for making a cut on a miter or cutoff saw?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** With power disconnected, set the angle and verify the blade or wheel, guard, fence, and clamp. Position and secure the material, reconnect power, raise the arm, start the saw, and wait for full speed. Lower the arm steadily through the work, release the trigger, and keep the arm controlled until rotation stops.

</details>

### 2.5.1 Table Saw Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** How do you reduce blade-contact, binding, and kickback hazards on a table saw?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Use the correct sharp blade, working guard, proper blade height, clean table, and required push stick. Keep stock flat against the table and rip fence, stand out of the kickback line, and never reach near or over a moving blade. Disconnect power before adjustments or maintenance, and do not trap a crosscut piece between the blade and fence.

</details>

### 2.5.2 Ripping Wood with a Table Saw

**Front:** What sequence keeps a rip cut controlled from setup through completion?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Disconnect power, install a rip blade, set blade height and fence, then reconnect power. Place flat stock against the fence without touching the blade, let the blade reach full speed, feed steadily while maintaining fence contact, and use a push stick whenever hands could approach the blade. Turn the saw off after the cut.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
Start a circular saw with the blade clear and let it reach **full speed**. A **jigsaw** is suited to delicate work in thin material. A portable band-saw blade travels continuously around **guides**. A **compound miter saw** pivots horizontally and vertically, and feeding a table saw before full speed can cause **kickback and serious injury**.
:::

## Section 3: Grinders and Oscillating Multi-Tools

### 3.1.0 Types of Grinders

**Front:** How do angle, end, detail, and bench grinders differ?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Angle grinders remove heavy material from surfaces with a disc at a right angle to the motor. End grinders smooth inside surfaces such as pipe. Detail or die grinders use small points for intricate metalwork. Bench grinders are stationary machines, usually with two wheels, for grinding, rust removal, buffing, and tool-edge maintenance.

</details>

### Bench Grinders and Tool Rests

**Front:** Why are wheel ratings and tool-rest clearances important on a bench grinder?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A wheel used above its rated maximum speed can break apart. Its rating must equal or exceed the grinder's maximum rpm. Keep the tool rest about 1/8 inch (3 mm) from the wheel and the spark arrestor about 1/4 inch (6 mm) away, using the manufacturer's specification; disconnect power before adjustment.

</details>

### 3.1.1 Grinder Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** What common checks prevent grinder wheel failures, fires, and loss of control?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Wear eye protection and a face shield as required, remove loose clothing and jewelry, inspect wheels for cracks or chips, match wheel type and rated maximum speed to the grinder, use the manufacturer-provided guard, secure the work, and direct sparks away from people and combustibles. Use two hands on portable grinders and wait for a complete stop before setting one down.

</details>

### 3.1.2 Operating Grinders

**Front:** What operating habits protect the wheel, workpiece, and operator while grinding?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Secure the work, establish stable footing, grip the tool correctly, let the wheel reach full speed, and use light controlled pressure without overloading the motor. On a bench grinder, support work on the rest, work only on the wheel face unless designed otherwise, and cool metal to preserve its temper. Stop the wheel fully before cleanup.

</details>

### 3.2.0 Grinder Attachments and Accessories

**Front:** How should grinder guards, pads, wheels, brushes, and bits be selected?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Match every guard, adapter, backing pad, disc, brush, wheel, or bit to the grinder, arbor, job, material, and rpm. Wire and cup brushes remove rust and scale; flap discs grind and blend; cutting wheels cut; buffing wheels polish; coring bits make holes; and burr bits remove rough edges. Follow the accessory manufacturer's limits and required authorization.

</details>

### 3.3.0 Oscillating Multi-Tools

**Front:** What makes an oscillating multi-tool useful, and what jobs can it perform?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** An OMT moves small attachments rapidly back and forth and fits into spaces where larger tools cannot. With the correct attachment it can make small cuts, undercut trim, cut drywall, metal, or PVC, scrape grout, remove adhesive or paint, and sand or prepare surfaces.

</details>

### 3.3.1 Oscillating Multi-Tool Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** What checks apply before operating an oscillating multi-tool?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Wear required PPE, disconnect the OMT from its power source before changing attachments or maintenance, install an attachment designed for the task, and clamp the workpiece. Keep hands, loose clothing, hair, and jewelry away from the attachment, and remember that its high-speed motion may be difficult to see.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
A stationary two-wheel machine is a **bench grinder**. Rotary **burr bits** remove rough edges, and an oscillating multi-tool is especially useful because it can **fit into tight places**.
:::

## Section 4: Miscellaneous Power Tools

### 4.1.0 Power Nailers

**Front:** How do pneumatic and cordless nailers work, and what basic interlock is expected on a pneumatic nailer?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Pneumatic nailers use compressed air and job-specific nails; cordless nailers use battery power and avoid an air hose. A pneumatic nailer is designed not to fire unless its contact tip is pressed against the work and the trigger is operated. Never defeat a safety device or exceed the specified pressure.

</details>

### Powder-Actuated Fastening Tools

**Front:** Why are powder-actuated fastening tools treated differently from ordinary nailers?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** They use a powder charge to drive steel pins or studs into concrete, masonry, or steel and can behave like firearms. Operators must be trained and certified for the specific manufacturer's tool, use the correct load and fastener, check local restrictions, and control penetration and the area beyond the work.

</details>

### 4.1.1 Power Nailer Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** What are the essential controls for pneumatic, cordless, and powder-actuated nailers?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Wear eye protection, read the manual, select the correct tool and fastener, inspect behind the surface, and never point a nailer at yourself or anyone else. Treat it as loaded, keep hands out of the line of fire, load only when ready, disconnect power before loading or repair, and never use a powder-actuated tool without required training and certification.

</details>

### 4.1.2 Operating Power Nailers

**Front:** What preparation and shutdown sequence applies when using a power nailer?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Put on PPE, disconnect the energy source, inspect the tool, and load the specified fastener and charge in the required order. Reconnect or pressurize within the stated range, test on suitable scrap, hold the tool squarely and firmly to the work, and fire only into a verified safe surface. When finished, disconnect air or battery and remove powder charges.

</details>

### 4.2.0 Hydraulic Jacks

**Front:** What can hydraulic jacks do, and how are their capacities described?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Hydraulic jacks apply large controlled force to lift, move, position, straighten, or bend heavy loads. A pump pressurizes fluid to move a cylinder or ram. Cylinders are rated by lifting capacity and stroke, the distance the cylinder can move the load; both ratings and the intended orientation must suit the task.

</details>

### 4.2.1 Hydraulic Jack Safety and Maintenance

**Front:** How must a load be prepared, lifted, and supported when using a hydraulic jack?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Use a correctly rated inspected jack on a solid, level base; center the load, clear the area, chock possible movement, and monitor fluid and hose condition. Stay clear, do not use a cheater bar, and block the load progressively. Never leave a jack under a load as a support; install suitable blocking before removing the jack.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
A pneumatic nailer fires only when its contact tip is **pressed against the material** and its trigger is operated. Place a hydraulic jack on a **solid, level surface**, stay clear of the load, and support the raised load with proper blocking rather than the jack.
:::
