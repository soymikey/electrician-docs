# Module 00108: Basic Employability Skills

This page organizes Module 00108 into 31 active-recall cards. Answer each **Front** question before expanding the **Back** to check your response.

:::info[How to review]
Mark a card **Know** if you can explain it fully and give a workplace example without looking. Mark it **Unsure** if you remember only part of the answer, and **Do not know** if you cannot answer. Repeat the **Unsure** and **Do not know** cards first.
:::

## Section 1: Opportunities in the Construction Industry

### 1.0.0 Opportunities in the Construction Industry

**Front:** What kinds of career opportunities exist in construction, and who is responsible for advancement?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Construction offers blue-collar craft positions and white-collar professional positions in many specialties and locations. Opportunities also exist in fields such as wind energy, powerlines, welding, crane operation, and instrumentation. Each worker must prepare, recognize opportunities, and take the steps needed to advance.

</details>

### 1.1.0 The Construction Business

**Front:** Why does a large construction project require many specialized roles?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Projects combine design, engineering, management, site preparation, structural work, utilities, building systems, controls, and finish work. Architects, engineers, project managers, equipment operators, concrete workers, ironworkers, welders, electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, pipefitters, carpenters, and other specialists must coordinate their work.

</details>

### Understanding an Employer

**Front:** Why should you research a company's mission, policies, and organization before applying?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A mission statement explains how a company does business, while policies and procedures explain what it expects from employees. Knowing the company's work, values, structure, and safety record helps you decide whether it fits your goals and prepares you to answer interview questions effectively.

</details>

### 1.2.0 Entering the Construction Workforce

**Front:** What makes a construction resume effective?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Use a readable font, match the objective and skills to keywords in the job description, list the most recent work first, and describe skills with short statements and bullets. Include relevant credentials, accurate contact information, a clear timeline, and references, then have someone proofread it.

</details>

### Finding Job Opportunities

**Front:** Why is networking important during a job search, and who can serve as a reference?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Many openings are never publicly advertised, so contacts at target companies, family, friends, coworkers, supervisors, and social networks may reveal opportunities that job boards do not. A reference is someone who can confirm your skills, experience, and work habits; provide current contact information for both professional and personal references.

</details>

### Evaluating and Accepting a Position

**Front:** What should you evaluate before accepting a construction job?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Consider salary, benefits, duties, challenge level, company reputation, coworkers, travel, training, advancement, and safety record. Be prepared to provide identification, credentials, qualification tests, and drug-test results when required, and use an organizational chart to understand responsibilities and reporting relationships.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
A **project manager** plans, coordinates, and controls project progress. A valid resume practice is to use chronological order with the current or most recent job first. The module review also identifies networking with family and friends as one of the most productive ways to find opportunities, and a person who verifies your skills and work habits is a **reference**.
:::

## Section 2: Critical Thinking and Problem Solving

### 2.1.1 Barriers to Problem Solving

**Front:** What three barriers commonly prevent effective problem solving?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Closed-mindedness, personality conflicts, and fear of change. Stay open to unfamiliar ideas, separate the value of a message from your feelings about the messenger, and treat change as an opportunity rather than rejecting it because the outcome is uncertain.

</details>

### 2.2.0 Solving Problems Using Critical Thinking

**Front:** What is the five-step problem-solving process?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Define the problem; analyze alternatives; choose a solution and plan it; implement the solution and monitor results; then evaluate the final result. If the result is unacceptable, return to the earlier steps and try another solution.

</details>

### 2.2.1 Defining the Problem

**Front:** What must you establish before trying to solve a problem?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Identify the exact difference between the present condition and the required condition. Verify facts and scope so you do not spend time solving the wrong problem or a problem that does not actually exist.

</details>

### 2.2.2 Analyzing the Alternatives

**Front:** How should possible solutions be developed and compared?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Gather reliable information, ask experts and coworkers for input, and create several alternatives. Compare cost, time, quality, and both short- and long-term consequences. Team input improves the chance of finding an option you would not identify alone.

</details>

### 2.2.3 Choose a Solution and Plan

**Front:** What must an implementation plan specify after a solution is selected?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Identify required tools and materials, list the tasks, assign responsibility, put the tasks in a logical sequence, and estimate how long each will take. The plan should make the selected solution practical to carry out.

</details>

### 2.2.4 Implement and Monitor

**Front:** Why must a solution be monitored after implementation?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** A logical plan may produce unexpected results. Following it closely shows whether work is progressing toward the desired outcome and allows problems or deviations to be corrected before they grow.

</details>

### 2.2.5 Evaluate the Final Result

**Front:** What should you do after the solution has been completed?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Compare the result with the desired condition and review what the process taught you. If the solution failed, return to the selection and planning stage, choose another option, and implement a revised plan without letting the first failure destroy your confidence.

</details>

### Planning and Scheduling Problems

**Front:** How should a worker prepare a task within the overall project schedule?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Understand the assigned scope, where the work begins and ends, required materials, individual steps, and their sequence. Also identify predecessor and successor tasks, because delays in one activity can prevent other crafts from starting their work.

</details>

### 2.3.1 Materials

**Front:** What material problems can delay a project, and why may substitution take time?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Wrong quantities or types, late deliveries, backorders, supplier closure, waste, theft, and vandalism can create shortages. A substitute not listed in the contract documents normally must pass through an approval process before installation.

</details>

### 2.3.2 Equipment

**Front:** What equipment problems can stop several crafts at once?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Equipment may be unavailable, lack a qualified operator, break down, or remain in maintenance longer than expected. Because several crews may depend on one machine, the absence of equipment such as a crane can stop ironworkers, welders, and other trades together.

</details>

### 2.3.3 Tools

**Front:** How are workers and employers responsible for tool availability?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Workers are commonly expected to provide and maintain specified everyday tools, while the employer usually provides expensive or specialized tools. Breakage, damage, loss, theft, missing support equipment, or a lack of trained users can all delay work.

</details>

### 2.3.4 Labor

**Front:** What labor problems affect cost and schedule?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Tardiness, absenteeism, too few workers, missing qualifications or experience, poor task assignments, idle time, and lack of guidance can delay a project. Planners estimate the number and skills needed, while foremen ensure the right people are assigned to the right work.

</details>

### 2.3.5 Handling Delays

**Front:** What should you do when you identify a possible project delay?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Look ahead, recognize recurring risks, and notify your supervisor immediately. Know your responsibility, use an existing contingency plan when available, solve the issue yourself only when assigned to do so, and keep supervision informed about sensitive situations.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
When new information conflicts with what you know, question it and separate fact from fiction. Monitor an implemented solution to confirm that it produces the intended result. A material substitution not specified in the contract will likely require approval. The module review identifies **fear of change** as a problem-solving barrier, monitoring as the step after implementation, and labor as a potentially major project cost.
:::

## Section 3: Relationship and Social Skills

### 3.1.1 Personal Habits

**Front:** Which personal habits communicate dependability and professionalism?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Arrive on time, keep commitments, organize tools and work areas, plan the day, stay technically qualified, help without overcommitting, act honestly, follow safety and confidentiality rules, and meet dress and grooming requirements. Do not attempt unfamiliar hazardous work without proper qualification.

</details>

### 3.1.2 Work Ethic

**Front:** What does a strong work ethic look like, and what is proper initiative?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Give a fair day's work, do your best, solve appropriate problems without constant supervision, and look for useful work after finishing a task. Initiative does not mean ignoring instructions; bring a possible improvement to the supervisor and obtain approval before changing the required process.

</details>

### 3.1.3 Tardiness and Absenteeism

**Front:** How do tardiness and absenteeism affect a construction project, and how can they be prevented?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Late or missing workers force expensive schedule and crew adjustments and burden coworkers. Allow enough travel time, follow the company's reporting policy, keep the supervisor informed, explain late arrivals promptly, and do not abuse lunch or break privileges.

</details>

### 3.2.1 Resolving Conflicts with Co-Workers

**Front:** What process should coworkers use to resolve a conflict?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Admit that a conflict exists, describe each person's view, identify its cause, and discuss solutions calmly and respectfully. Compromise when necessary, act on the agreement, and check whether it works. Apologize when wrong and ask a supervisor for help if the conflict cannot be resolved.

</details>

### 3.2.2 Resolving Conflicts with Supervisors

**Front:** How should you discuss a disagreement with a supervisor?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Organize the facts first, request a convenient private meeting, and speak calmly without sarcasm, threats, or unsupported accusations. Explain possible solutions and their benefits, allow the supervisor to decide, and follow the final decision. Significant unresolved issues may need Human Resources.

</details>

### 3.3.1 Offering Constructive Criticism

**Front:** How should constructive criticism be offered?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Speak privately, use positive and supportive language, give facts instead of personal opinions, focus on the work rather than the person, and suggest an alternative. Correct unsafe or improper behavior early, avoid constant criticism, and offer a sincere compliment before the correction when appropriate.

</details>

### 3.3.2 Receiving Constructive Criticism

**Front:** How should you respond to constructive criticism?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Treat it as an opportunity to learn, listen without becoming defensive, accept responsibility, and ask for specific changes or details when the advice is unclear. Improve your performance when the advice is valid; if it is incorrect, explain your disagreement clearly and respectfully.

</details>

### 3.3.3 Destructive Criticism

**Front:** What is destructive criticism, and how should you handle it?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Destructive criticism is delivered to hurt rather than help. Stay calm, avoid replying with another attack, explain how the delivery affected you, and ask for positive suggestions if a legitimate performance issue exists beneath the hostile tone.

</details>

### 3.4.1 Harassment

**Front:** What behavior can qualify as workplace harassment, and what should a worker do?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Harassment includes hostile or offensive behavior connected to protected or personal characteristics, sexual harassment, bullying, and cyberbullying. Sexual harassment is illegal and should be reported immediately. Report other harassment through company procedures and carefully document events so responsible authorities can act.

</details>

### 3.4.2 Drug and Alcohol Abuse

**Front:** Why do construction employers enforce a zero-tolerance policy for drugs and alcohol?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Impairment can cause fatal accidents, injuries, poor decisions, and defective work. A zero-tolerance policy applies consequences even to violations considered minor in order to protect the workplace. Testing may occur before employment, randomly, or after an accident; workers who need help should seek available counseling and support.

</details>

### Teamwork

**Front:** What behaviors make someone an effective construction team member?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Focus on the shared goal, follow leadership, use your strengths, accept limitations, keep a positive attitude, trust others to perform their roles, help coworkers, finish work on time, share credit, and accept responsibility for mistakes. Experienced workers should teach patiently and by example.

</details>

### 3.5.1 Leadership Skills

**Front:** What is leadership, and which leadership styles does the module review identify?

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**Back:** Leadership is setting an example for others through authority and responsibility. Experience, credentials, hard work, and dedication can lead from apprentice to team leader, foreman, supervisor, project manager, or business owner. The module review identifies the three leadership styles as **hands-off, democratic, and autocratic**.

</details>

:::tip[Section Review focus]
Positive and negative interactions affect **relationships**. Working without constant supervision shows **initiative**, and solving disagreements is **conflict resolution**. Constructive criticism should include a helpful alternative and should be delivered positively. **Harassment** is the broad term for prohibited negative social behavior, and zero tolerance applies to drug and alcohol abuse. Effective teams are goal-oriented, and career advancement requires leadership skills.
:::
