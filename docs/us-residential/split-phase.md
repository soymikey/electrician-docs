# US Residential Split-Phase Power

Most US homes receive split-phase service from a transformer with a center-tapped secondary. Each hot leg to neutral is typically about 120V, while the two hot legs together provide about 240V.

:::danger Safety boundary
Residential panels, 240V circuits, service equipment, grounding, and bonding are governed by code and licensing rules. Treat this page as a concept map, not permission to work on live equipment.
:::

## What to remember

- 120V loads usually connect hot-to-neutral.
- 240V loads connect hot-to-hot and may or may not need a neutral.
- Neutral is the grounded circuit conductor and carries normal return current.
- Equipment grounding conductors are for fault current, not normal load current.
- Split phase is not three-phase power.
