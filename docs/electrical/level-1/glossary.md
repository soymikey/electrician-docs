# Glossary

Twelfth edition *Electrical, Level 1*. Extracted from the glossary at the back of the book.

197 entries, in textbook order. Not translated.

**Alternating current (AC):** An electric current that changes direction and magnitude over time.

**Ammeter:** An electrical test instrument designed to read current.

**Ampacity:** The maximum current in amperes (amps or A) that a conductor can carry continuously under the conditions of use without exceeding its temperature rating.

**Ampere (A):** The basic unit of measurement for electric current, represented by the letter *A*.

**Appliances:** Nonindustrial equipment installed or connected as a unit to perform one or more specific functions, such as baking, frying, or air conditioning. Other examples include washing machines, blenders, and microwave ovens. Appliances may be stationary or portable.

**Arc flash:** The light and heat produced from an electric arc that has enough electrical energy to cause substantial damage, fire, and even injury.

**Arc flash boundary (AFB):** An approach distance limit from exposed energized electrical conductors or circuit parts. A person could receive a second-degree burn if an electric arc flash were to occur within this limit.

**Arc flash risk assessment:** A study investigating a worker’s potential exposure to arc flash energy. The assessment is conducted by a qualified worker for injury prevention, determination of safe work practices, and appropriate levels of PPE.

**Architectural drawings:** Working drawings that consist of site plans, floor plans, elevations, sectional views, details, and other information necessary for the construction of a building.

**Architect’s scale:** A special ruler that allows you to measure distances on drawings as real-world distances in feet, inches, and fractions of an inch. An architect’s scale measures distances in inches.

**Arc rating:** The maximum incident energy resistance demonstrated by a material (or a layered system of materials) prior to material breakdown, or at the onset of a second-degree skin burn. Expressed in joules per centimeter squared (J/cm2) or calories per centimeter squared (cal/cm2).

**Articles:** The main topics of the *NEC*®, beginning with **NEC Article 90**, *Introduction*, and ending with **NEC Article 830**, *Network-Powered Broadband Communications Systems*.

**As-built drawings:** A marked up set of drawings, also called *red lines*, made at the completion of the project, showing all changes made during the construction project.

**Atom:** The smallest particle in which an element may be divided and still retain the properties of that element.

**Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ):** An organization or person responsible for enforcing code requirements and/or adopted standards, as well as approving the related equipment, materials, or installation procedures.

**Auxiliary gutter:** Sheet metal or flame-retardant nonmetallic enclosure with hinged or removable covers used as supplemental and expansive wire space near service entrances, metering centers, and similar locations.

**Back-to-back bend:** Any bend formed by two 90° bends with a straight section of conduit between the bends.

**Backfeed:** The reverse flow of electrical power in a distribution system, caused by power introduced into the system from outside sources, such as an emergency generator or a solar photovoltaic (PV) system. Backfeeds can also result from equipment overloads.

**Battery:** A direct current (DC) voltage source consisting of two or more cells that convert chemical energy into electrical energy.

**Bend protractor:** Tool used to measure bend angle.

**Blueprints:** Exact copies or reproductions of original drawings.

**Bonding:** The electrical connection of all exposed conductive items not designed to carry electricity to protect against electric shock potential. This puts all the connected components at the same electrical potential in case they become energized.

**Bonding jumper:** A bare or green insulated conductor used to ensure electrical conductivity between metal parts connected electrically. Bonding jumpers often go from a bonding bushing to the service equipment enclosure. It also provides a path around concentric knockouts in an enclosure wall and can be used to bond one raceway to another.

**Bonding wire:** A wire used to support a continuous grounding path between electrical equipment and ground.

**Branch circuit:** The portion of a wiring system extending beyond the final overcurrent protective device (OCPD) in a circuit.

**Cable:** One or more conductors installed inside a common protective outer jacket or sheath.

**Cable tray:** An assembly of various components forming a structure to secure and support conductors, cables, and other forms of raceway.

**Capacitance:** The ability of a device (such as a capacitor) to store electrical energy. The unit of measurement for capacitance is the farad (F) or microfarad (µF).

**Change order:** A formal document from the project manager or owner, specifying one or more changes to the drawings, specifications, or project scope. It will also state any changes in project cost.

**Chapters:** Contain a group of articles related to a broad category. Nine chapters form the overall structure of the *NEC*®.

**Charged:** Possessing some volume of electricity that is either positive or negative in nature. Like charges repel each other, while opposite charges attract each other.

**Cinder concrete:** A lightweight concrete made using coal cinders as an aggregate with Portland cement and water.

**Cinder fill:** A land-filling bulk product primarily consisting of by-products from coal combustion.

**Circuit:** A complete path for current flow.

**Circular mils (cmils):** Units of measure based on a circle with a diameter of 1 mil, or 0.001". One kcmil is equal to 1,000 cmils.

**Competent persons:** Workers who can identify hazards and take the corrective action necessary to address them.

**Concentric bends:** Complimentary bends made in parallel runs of conduit that have an increasing bend radius, keeping the conduit runs parallel with each other as they change direction.

**Conductors:** Materials through which an electric current flows easily.

**Conduit:** A round raceway, similar to pipe, to route and protect conductors.

**Conduit body:** An electrical fitting used in place of elbows, tees, and similar fittings to provide an access point to the conductors inside; also called a *condulet* or *pulling ell*.

**Connectors:** Devices used to securely fasten conduit or cable to an outlet box, cabinet, or other enclosure.

**Continuity:** A term that refers to a complete (unbroken) circuit capable of conducting current. Such a circuit is called a *closed circuit*.

**Contour lines:** Curving lines on a site plan that follow a given elevation. The space between contour lines tells the slope of the property; the slope is steep when contour lines are close together, and fairly level when they are widely separated.

**Corona discharge:** A unique electrical event associated with high-voltage conductors and equipment, appearing as a blue or violet glow accompanied by crackling or hissing. Corona discharge occurs when the air surrounding a high-voltage surface becomes ionized and conductive, creating an area of plasma and corrosive gases around it.

**Cosecant (csc):** In a right triangle, the ratio of the length of the hypotenuse to the length of the side opposite the angle of interest.

**Cosine (cos):** In a right triangle, the ratio of the length of the side adjacent to the angle of interest to the length of the hypotenuse.

**Current:** The movement, or flow, of electrons in a circuit. The intensity of current flow, which is represented mathematically by the letter *I*, is measured in amperes (A).

**Detail drawing:** An enlarged, detailed view taken from an area of a drawing and shown in a separate view.

**Developed length:** The length of straight conduit needed to complete a bend at a given radius; the length of conduit consumed in the bend.

**Dielectric union:** A pipe or plumbing fittings used to separate metallic components and prevent current flow, including dissimilar metals that can develop a galvanic current and initiate corrosion in one of the metals.

**Dimensions:** Sizes or measurements printed on a drawing.

**Diode:** A common semiconductor component in electronic circuits with two terminals that typically allows the flow of current in only one direction.

**Direct current (DC):** An electric current that flows in one constant direction only.

**Dog leg:** Misalignment in conduit when the centerline that runs its length is not maintained through multiple bends, resulting in a crooked bend.

**Double-insulated/ungrounded tools:** Electrical tools that are constructed so that the case is insulated from electrical energy. The case is made of a nonconductive material.

**Dwelling unit:** A single living space designed for one or more people to permanently live, cook, and sleep, including the necessary sanitation utilities.

**Electrical box:** A metallic or nonmetallic box installed in an electrical wiring system to safely house electrical components and/ or securely mount devices and electrical cables. Specific uses include outlet boxes, junction boxes, device boxes, splice boxes, and pull boxes.

**Electrical drawing:** An illustration that conveys a large amount of precise and accurate information about an electrical system. It includes blocks, lines, symbols, dimensions, and notations.

**Electrical service:** The conductors and equipment connecting the serving utility to the wiring system of the premises.

**Electron:** A negatively charged particle that orbits the nucleus of an atom.

**Elevation view:** An architectural drawing showing height and width, but not depth; usually showing the front, rear, and sides of a building or object.

**Engineer’s scale:** A special ruler that allows you to measure distances on drawings as real-world distances in decimals. An engineer’s scale measures distances in decimal units.

**Equipment grounding conductor (EGC):** A conductive part, most often a wire, that connects normally noncurrent-carrying metal parts of equipment, such as the enclosure of an oven, to the grounding system.

**Error precursors:** Situations that put a worker at risk due to the demands of the task, conditions, worker attitude, and/or environment.

**Exceptions:** Exceptions follow the applicable sections of the *NEC*® and allow alternative methods to be used under specific conditions.

**Exothermic welding process:** A process that relies on a self-sustaining chemical reaction to weld conductors together, generating heat and melting the metal without the need for an external heat source. Also referred to as *thermite welding*, since thermite is often used to create the chemical reaction.

**Explosion proof:** Designed and constructed to withstand an internal explosion without creating an external explosion or fire.

**Feeders:** Any circuit conductors between the service equipment, the source of a separately derived system, or other power supply source and the final branch circuit overcurrent device.

**Fiber optic cable:** A type of cable that transmits data through light signals; used for purposes like networking and high-speed data communication.

**Fibrillation:** The uncoordinated quivering of the heart muscle caused by an alternating current (AC) electric shock.

**Fish tape:** Long, flat, or round heavy wire inserted through conduit, walls, and other inaccessible locations to connect to conductors and pull them through. Fish tapes may be made from conductive steel or nonconductive fiberglass or nylon.

**Fish tape:** Long, flat, or round heavy wire inserted through conduit, walls, and other inaccessible locations to connect to conductors and pull them through. Fish tapes may be made from steel or nonconductive fiberglass or nylon.

**Floor plan:** A drawing that depicts the horizontal cross section of a building taken at about window level. The floor plan is what would appear if the structure were viewed from above with the top portion removed.

**Follow bar:** The fixed channel of a bender against which the shoe forms the conduit bend.

**Footcandles:** A unit of measure used to quantify the amount of light reaching a surface, but not the light reflected from it.

**Frequency:** The number of cycles an alternating electric current, sound wave, or vibrating object completes over time. Frequency is expressed in hertz (Hz), with 1 Hz equal to 1 cycle per second.

**Gain (G):** The amount of conduit saved by bending on a radius as opposed to bending at a sharp angle.

**Gigaohms:** Units of resistance equal to 1,000,000,000 Ω; abbreviated as GΩ*.*

**Grounded conductor:** A conductor that is intentionally grounded; typically refers to the neutral conductor.

**Grounded tool:** An electric tool that has a power cord with a three-prong plug or some other mechanism, providing a low-impedance ground path for a ground fault current to return to its source. This setup allows an overcurrent device to operate in the event of a ground fault, which protects the user from shock.

**Ground fault circuit interrupter (GFCI):** A device that protects people and equipment by de-energizing a circuit within an established period of time when a ground fault current exceeds the values established for a Class A device (4–6 mA).

**Grounding:** Refers to the practice of connecting electrical equipment to the earth or a conductive connection to the earth.

**Grounding electrode:** A conductive object that creates a direct, reliable electrical connection to earth.

**Grounding electrode conductor (GEC):** A conductor that connects the system grounded neutral conductor or the connected equipment to a grounding electrode or system.

**Handhole enclosure:** An enclosure used with underground systems to provide access for installation and maintenance.

**Handy box:** A single-gang box, also known as a *utility box*. It encloses receptacles or wall switches on concrete block construction and is nongangable. It is commonly mounted on masonry surfaces, but can also be recessed-mounted.

**Hickey bender:** A special bender with a short shoe, used to create segmented bends.

**Hoistways:** Shafts, hatches, and similar vertical passages designed for elevators, dumbwaiters, and similar hoisting applications.

**Hot stick:** A manually operated insulated tool designed for disconnecting and connecting switches, removing and inserting fuses, applying and removing temporary grounds, and testing.

**Hub:** A fitting similar to a box connector but with larger flanges and typically watertight, used to connect IMC and RMC to an enclosure.

**Incident energy:** The amount of thermal energy impressed on a surface at a certain distance from the source of an electric arc. Incident energy is typically expressed in calories per centimeter squared (cal/cm2).

**Inductive loads:** Electrical loads that convert electrical energy into a magnetic field to operate, with some energy being stored in the magnetic field.

**Informational notes:** Explanatory material that follows specific *NEC*® sections.

**Institute for Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE):** A professional organization that develops international standards for electronics, telecommunications, information technology, and power generation products and services.

**Insulator:** A material that does not allow significant electric current flow.

**International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC):** An international organization that develops consensus standards for all electrical and electronic technologies.

**Ion:** An atom with a negative or positive charge due to the gain or loss of electrons.

**Junction box:** A box through which conductors, raceways, or cables are protected and organized at critical points in a wiring system. Electrical conductors can be spliced inside this box to extend electrical branches.

**Kick:** A single bend made at less than 90° to change the direction of the conduit.

**Kick:** A single bend made at less than 90° to change the direction of the conduit.

**Kiloohms:** Units of resistance equal to 1,000 Ω; abbreviated as kΩ.

**Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL):** A natural law stating that the total current entering a node or junction point in a circuit must equal the total current leaving that node or point.

**Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL):** A natural law stating that the sum of all the voltage drops in a circuit is equal to the source voltage of the circuit.

**Leg length:** The distance from the end of a straight section of conduit to a bend, measured to the centerline, inside surface, or outside surface of the material depending on the application. The *leg* is the opposite end of the conduit from a stub-up.

**Limited approach boundary:** An approach distance limit from an exposed energized electrical conductor or circuit part within which a shock hazard exists.

**Luminaires:** Complete light assemblies including the lamp or bulb and a means of connecting the power source. The assembly might also include protection for the lamp and lenses to distribute the light.

**Lux:** A metric unit of measure used to quantify light intensity.

**Megohms:** Units of resistance equal to 1,000,000 Ω; abbreviated as MΩ.

**Mouse:** A cylinder of foam rubber that fits inside the conduit and is propelled by compressed air or a vacuum, pulling a connected line or tape behind it.

**National Electrical Manufacturers Association (NEMA):** The association that maintains and improves the quality and reliability of electrical products.

**National Fire Protection Association (NFPA):** The publisher of the *NEC*®. The NFPA develops codes and standards to minimize the possibility and effects of fire.

**Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratories (NRTLs):** Product safety certification laboratories that are responsible for testing and certifying electrical equipment.

**Neutron:** An electrically neutral particle (neither positive nor negative) that has the same mass as a proton and is found in the nucleus (center) of an atom.

**Nucleus:** The central portion of an atom, which contains the protons and neutrons.

**Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA):** The US government agency established to ensure a safe and healthy workplace.

**Offset:** A change of direction made with two equal bends in opposing directions, usually in quick succession, made to align the conduit with a point of connection or avoid an obstruction.

**Offset:** A change of direction made with two equal bends in opposing directions, usually in quick succession, made to align the conduit with a point of connection or avoid an obstruction.

**Ohm (Ω):** The basic unit of measure for electrical resistance, represented by the symbol Ω.

**Ohmmeter:** An electrical test instrument designed to measure resistance to current flow and to determine whether a circuit is complete or intact (continuity).

**Ohm’s law:** A fundamental law stating that the current in a circuit is directly proportional to the voltage and inversely proportional to the resistance. Stated mathematically, the current (*I*) equals the voltage (*E*) divided by the resistance (*R*).

**On-the-job learning (OJL):** Learning a job as an apprentice under the supervision of journey-level workers. Also called *on-the- job training (OJT)*.

**One-line diagram:** A drawing that shows, by means of lines and symbols, the path of an electrical circuit or system of circuits along with the various circuit components. Also called a *single-line diagram*.

**One-shot shoe:** A large bending shoe that is designed to make 90° bends in one motion.

**Outlet box:** A metallic or nonmetallic box installed in an electrical wiring system to protect electrical devices.

**Parallel circuits:** Electrical circuits with more than one path for current to follow, where each load is connected directly to the voltage source.

**Parallel conductors:** Multiple conductors of the same size and construction connected at both ends of a run to create a larger current-carrying path, used to create additional ampacity without using a single much larger conductor.

**Parts:** Certain articles in the *NEC*® are divided into parts that cover a specific topic. Parts are numbered with Roman numerals (e.g., **NEC Article 250, Part IX**, *Instruments, Meters, and Relays*).

**Plan:** A drawing made as though the viewer were looking straight down on an object from above.

**Polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs):** Toxic chemicals that may be contained in liquids used to cool certain types of large transformers, capacitors, and large switches.

**Power:** The rate at which electrical energy is transferred or converted into heat or work; the rate at which electricity is consumed. Electrical power is measured in watts (W).

**Power riser diagram:** A single-line block diagram used to indicate the arrangement of electric service equipment, service conductors and feeders, and subpanels. Notes are used on power riser diagrams to identify the equipment; indicate the size of conduit; show the number, size, and type of conductors; and list related materials. A panelboard schedule is usually included with power riser diagrams to indicate the exact components (panel type and size), fuses, circuit breakers, etc. that are contained in each panelboard.

**Proton:** The smallest positively charged particle of an atom. Protons are found in the nucleus (center) of an atom.

**Pull box:** A box-like enclosure made of sheet metal. It is used in conduit runs to make it easier to pull cables from point to point in long runs. It can also help you install conduit support bushings. This part supports the weight of long riser cables or helps create turns in multiple conduit runs.

**Qualified persons:** People who have the necessary training or certifications and have demonstrated their knowledge and ability to resolve problems related to the work or project.

**Raceway:** An enclosed path designed to route and protect conductors, cables, and busbars.

**Raceway system:** An enclosure that houses the conductors in an electrical system.

**Radius:** The distance from the center of a circle to the perimeter. In conduit bending, the radius refers to the distance from the center of an imaginary circle to the centerline of the bent conduit.

**Raintight:** Something that is built or protected to keep out water during heavy rain under specific conditions.

**Ram travel:** The distance the ram of a hydraulic bender travels to make a bend at a given angle.

**Reamer:** A tool used to smooth the edges of conduit, pipe, or tubing after it has been cut.

**Request for information:** A formal document from the contractor seeking clarification when errors, omissions, and discrepancies are found in or between construction documents.

**Resistance:** An electrical property that opposes the flow of current through a circuit. Resistance is represented mathematically by the letter *R* and is measured in ohms (Ω).

**Resistive loads:** Electrical loads that typically convert electricity to heat or light while the voltage and current remain in phase with each other.

**Resistor:** A device in a circuit that resists the flow of electrons.

**Restricted approach boundary:** An approach distance limit from an exposed energized electrical conductor or circuit part within which there is an increased likelihood of electric shock.

**Rise:** The height of a stub-up, measured from the bottom of the conduit to the destination of the stub-up.

**Rough-in:** The beginning stage of wiring when electricians install the panelboard, wiring, and boxes.

**Roughing in:** The first stage of an electrical installation when the basic components, such as raceway, cable, and boxes, are installed while the walls and ceiling remain open. This work must be completed before these areas are closed and finishes are applied.

**Saddle:** A three- or four-bend change of direction to avoid an obstacle and then bring the conduit back to its original plane.

**Scale:** On a drawing, the relationship between an object’s actual size and drawn size. Scale also refers to the measuring tool used to determine this relationship.

**Schedule:** A systematic method of presenting equipment lists on a drawing in tabular form.

**Schematic diagram:** A detailed diagram showing complicated circuits, such as control circuits. A schematic diagram has no scale but shows components and their electrical connections.

**Schematic diagrams:** Electrical drawings in which symbols are used to represent the components in a system; also referred to simply as *schematics*.

**Sectional view:** A cutaway drawing that shows the inside of an object or building.

**Sections:** In the *NEC*®, parts and articles are divided into sections. Each section is numbered using the article number, a period, and then the section number. For example, **NEC Section 501.20**.

**Segmented bends:** Bends formed by a series of smaller bends of a few degrees each rather than a single bending motion. This allows bends with a larger radius.

**Segment shoe:** A smaller bending shoe designed specifically for fabricating segmented bends.

**Semiconductor:** A material that has electrical properties between those of a conductor and an insulator. Semiconductors are used to control current flow in electronic equipment and other applications.

**Series-parallel circuits:** Electrical circuits with a combination of series circuits and parallel circuits.

**Series circuits:** Electrical circuits with only one path for current to follow.

**Service-entrance conductors:** The conductors between the point of termination of the overhead service drop or underground service lateral and the main disconnecting device in the building.

**Service drop:** Overhead conductors through which electrical service is supplied.

**Service equipment:** The equipment where the utility service conductors terminate, including items such as a service disconnect and overcurrent protection, that provide a means of service control.

**Service lateral:** Underground conductors through which electrical service is supplied.

**Service point:** The point of connection between the facilities of the serving utility and the premises wiring.

**Shoe:** The U-shaped, radiused channel that fits around the conduit and, when rotated, forces the material to bend while maintaining its circular shape.

**Shop drawing:** A drawing that is usually developed by manufacturers, fabricators, or contractors to show specific dimensions and other important information concerning a particular piece of equipment and its installation methods.

**Single-line block diagram:** A single-line diagram used to show electrical equipment and related connections.

**Site plan:** A drawing showing the location of one or more buildings on the building site. Such drawings frequently show topographical lines, electrical and communication lines, water and sewer lines, sidewalks, driveways, and similar information.

**Splice:** The connection of two or more conductors at a point other than their termination.

**Springback:** The degree to which a bent section of conduit tends to straighten after pressure is released. For example, a 90° bend, after pressure is released, might spring back to an angle of 85° when it is released from the bender.

**Strut:** Refers to various forms of metal U-shaped channel used in construction like metal lumber to construct pipe, conduit, and equipment supports and hangers. Strut may have a continuous string of holes or slots along the back. Unistrut® and B-Line® are common brands used in the electrical industry.

**Stub-up:** Another name for the rise in a section of conduit, although a stub-up is typically considered to be relatively short.

**Substation:** An assemblage of equipment (such as switches, interrupting devices, circuit breakers, buses, and transformers) through which electric energy is passed for the purpose of distributing, switching, or modifying an electrical path.

**Sweep:** A conduit bend with a longer radius than common bends.

**Take-up:** The amount that must be subtracted from the desired stub height to form the bend in the correct location, using a point of reference on the bender or bending shoe. Also referred to as the *bend deduction* or *deduct*.

**Tap:** Intermediate point on a main circuit where an additional wire is connected to supply current to another circuit. A tap is a type of splice.

**Transistor:** A common semiconductor component with three terminals that acts as a switch to control current flow or acts as a current amplifier.

**Transposing:** Determining the cross-sectional area of the conductor(s) within a cable to verify the required capacity of a raceway.

**Trim-out:** The stage of wiring when electricians install and terminate devices and fixtures.

**True north:** The direction indicated on construction drawings that shows how property lines and buildings are positioned relative to a compass or magnetic north.

**Ungrounded conductors:** Service and building conductors that carry most of the current to the loads and are not physically connected to a ground reference or neutral busbar.

**Unqualified person:** A person who has little or no training in avoiding electrical hazards when working on or near exposed energized parts.

**Valence shell:** The outermost shell, or *ring*, of electrons that orbit the nucleus of an atom.

**Volt (V):** The unit of measurement for voltage, represented by the letter *V*. 1 V is the electromotive force needed to push 1 ampere (A) of current through a resistance of 1 ohm (Ω).

**Voltage:** The driving force that makes current flow in an electrical circuit. Voltage, which is usually represented mathematically by the letter *E* (electromotive force), is also referred to as *electrical potential*.

**Voltage drops:** Changes in voltage across a component caused by the current flowing through it and the amount of resistance opposing current flow.

**Voltage imbalance:** For a three-phase power supply, the maximum deviation of the voltage of any phase from the average voltage of the three phases, divided by the average voltage. Voltage imbalance is generally expressed as a percentage, with 2% generally considered excessive.

**Voltage transient:** A temporary, unwanted voltage applied to an electrical circuit that ranges from a few volts to thousands of volts and lasts from microseconds up to several milliseconds.

**Voltmeter:** An electrical test instrument designed to measure voltage.

**Watertight:** Something that is built so that water will not get inside of it under specific conditions.

**Watt (W):** The basic unit of measure for electrical power, represented by the letter *W*. 1 W of power is the energy used by 1 volt (V) to move electricity at a rate of 1 ampere (A).

**Watt-hour (Wh):** Unit of measure for energy consumption, equal to 1 watt (W) of power consumed for 1 hour (h).

**Weatherproof:** Something that is built or protected so weather does not interfere with its safe operation.

**Wire grip:** A device used to secure a pulling rope or tape to conductors or cable; also referred to as a *cable grip*.

**Wireway:** Sheet metal or flame-retardant nonmetallic trough with hinged or fastened covers used to secure and house conductors and cables distributed throughout a facility.

**Written specifications:** Written descriptions of what is required by the owner, architect, and engineer in the way of materials and workmanship. Together with working drawings, the specifications form the basis of the contract requirements for construction.
