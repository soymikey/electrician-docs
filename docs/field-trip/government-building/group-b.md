|---|---|---|

| Feed pumps | Pump numbers shown on the panel | Send water into boilers; actual locations and routing need checking. |
| Switches and controls | Visible front panel | Provide pump and boiler-related interfaces. |
| Level sensing | Typical supporting equipment; not identified here | Boiler-level sensing establishes feed demand; tank-level sensing manages tank inventory. |

**What to look for**

Identify pump numbers and BOILER-1 / BOILER-2 labels. Panel labels identify intended duties, but do not show the complete piping.

**How it works**

Steam leaving a boiler reduces its water inventory. A typical feedwater system responds to boiler water level by starting or regulating a pump that sends water from a tank into the boiler. The pump must overcome boiler-side pressure and piping resistance. Boiler level and tank level are different control tasks.

Principle references: [Shipco boiler-feed equipment](https://www.shipcopumps.com/products/).

## Putting it together: how the group operates

Review the equipment and components above before following these general paths. This is not a verified site schematic or an operating procedure.

### Simplified operating paths

**Energy path — typical steam boiler:** Fuel and air burn → heat crosses heating surfaces into water → water produces steam → steam travels to heat-using equipment. Flue gas leaves through the venting system.

**Water path — general principle:** Feedwater tank → feed pump → boiler → steam → condensation at use points. Recoverable condensate returns through appropriate recovery equipment to the feedwater side; makeup water replaces losses. The complete site return route has not been confirmed.

**Control path — general principle:** Pressure and level measurements → controller decisions → firing or feedwater adjustments; the HMI displays information. Supporting pumps, chemicals and controls are not one pipe assembled in photo order.
