## 1. IMG_9446 · Glycol piping label {#photo-9446}

[![Glycol piping label · IMG_9446](/img/field-trip/government-building/IMG_9446.webp)](/img/field-trip/government-building/IMG_9446.webp)

**What it is**

A GLYCOL label and arrows identify a visible pipe segment associated with glycol solution.

**Main components**

| Component | Location / evidence | Function |
|---|---|---|
| GLYCOL label and arrows | Visible | Identify the labeled segment and indicated flow direction. |
| Pipe and insulation | Exterior visible | Carry fluid and reduce heat exchange. |
| Handwheel-operated valve | Visible | Provides a piping control interface; internal valve type is unconfirmed. |

**What to look for**

Use the arrows for the indicated segment’s flow direction; do not apply the label to every nearby pipe.

**How it works**

Appropriately formulated glycol-water mixtures lower freezing temperature and carry heat between equipment. A pump still supplies the movement. Concentration affects freeze protection, viscosity and heat-transfer performance, so more glycol is not automatically better.

Principle references: [Dow heat-transfer fluids](https://www.dow.com/en-us/market/mkt-building-construction/sub-build-heating-cooling-refrigeration/heat-transfer-fluids-calculators.html).

**Still to confirm**

Confirm glycol type, concentration and circuit extent; these cannot be inferred from color alone.

## 2. IMG_9445 · Circulation pumps and valves {#photo-9445}

[![Circulation pumps and valves · IMG_9445](/img/field-trip/government-building/IMG_9445.webp)](/img/field-trip/government-building/IMG_9445.webp)

**What it is**

A close-up shows pumps, drives, insulated piping, valves and flexible connections.

**Main components**

| Component | Location / evidence | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Pump and motor | Partly visible green assemblies; impellers hidden | Drive fluid circulation. |
| Drive | Exterior visible | Controls the associated motor. |
| Valves and flexible connections | Visible external accessories | Control piping and accommodate designed movement or vibration; exact types require checking. |
| Insulated piping and gauges | Visible external accessories | Carry fluid and provide pressure observation points. |

**What to look for**

Find the green pump bodies and large valve handles, then trace the adjacent piping. A handle alone does not establish the internal valve type.

**How it works**

The pump creates a pressure difference to move fluid. Isolation valves open or isolate pipe sections. Suitable flexible connections can accommodate designed movement or vibration. Insulation reduces heat transfer to the surroundings and can help control condensation under appropriate conditions.

Further reading: [IMG_9442 explanation](./group-e.md#photo-9442).

**Still to confirm**

Confirm valve and connector types and the actual circuit.

## Putting it together: how the group operates

Review the equipment and components above before following these general paths. This is not a verified site schematic or an operating procedure.

### Simplified operating paths

**Typical heat-transfer loop:** A pump moves heat-transfer fluid → fluid gains or releases heat at heat-transfer equipment → fluid returns to continue circulating.

The learning order goes from “what is in the pipe?” to “what moves it?” The GLYCOL arrows describe the labeled segment. The two photographs do not show the complete circuit, all fluid identities, actual equipment positions or connections to other groups.
